# Auction Participation Guide

> Detailed step-by-step instructions for participating in each major auction
> type. Loaded when the Deal Intelligence Report recommends a specific auction
> and the user needs to know how to register, bid, pay, and take possession.
> Companion to `auction-constraints.md` (which covers the risk/constraint
> side) and `sourcing-sources.toml` (which has the structured metadata).

## How to Use This File

1. Identify the auction platform from the Deal Intelligence Report
2. Find the matching section below (or the closest analog)
3. Walk the user through the steps for that platform
4. If the platform is not listed below, use the closest analog and note the
   gap

---

## General Auction Participation Flow

Most online auction platforms follow the same 5-step flow:

```
Register → Verify Identity → Browse/Search → Bid → Pay & Pick Up
```

The details vary by platform. Below are platform-specific instructions.

---

## GSA Auctions (gsaauctions.gov)

**What they sell**: Federal surplus — vehicles, equipment, real property,
electronics, furniture, general items.

### Registration
1. Go to [gsaauctions.gov](https://gsaauctions.gov/)
2. Click "Register" and create a free account
3. Provide your Social Security Number (SSN) or Employer Identification
   Number (EIN) — required for tax purposes
4. Verify your identity (the system checks against government records)
5. Once approved, you can bid on any open auction

### Bidding
1. Browse auctions by category or search by keyword
2. Each listing has a minimum bid and bid increment
3. Place your bid; the system shows the current high bid
4. GSA Auctions uses a **sealed-bid** format for some sales and an
   **ascending-bid** (English auction) format for others — check the
   listing type
5. For sealed-bid sales: your bid is your best and final offer; the
   highest bid wins
6. For ascending-bid sales: you can bid multiple times; the auction ends
   at a specified time and the highest bid wins

### Payment
1. If you win, you'll receive an email with payment instructions
2. Payment is typically by ACH transfer, wire transfer, or credit card
   (some sales have credit card limits)
3. Payment is due within 2–7 business days of auction close
4. **No buyer's premium** — the winning bid is the price (plus any
   applicable sales tax)

### Pickup
1. Pickup location is specified in each listing
2. You must pick up within the timeframe stated in the listing (typically
   10–15 business days)
3. You must bring a government-issued ID and your payment confirmation
4. You are responsible for loading and transporting the item
5. GSA does not provide shipping — arrange your own transport

### Tips
- Check "Closed Auctions" to see historical sold prices for similar items
- Set up email alerts for new listings in your category
- Inspect items in person if the listing offers inspection dates
- GSA vehicles are federal fleet — high mileage but regularly maintained

## GSA Fleet Vehicle Sales (gsafleet.gov)

**What they sell**: Federal fleet vehicles (cars, trucks, vans).

### Registration
1. Go to [gsafleet.gov](https://www.gsafleet.gov/)
2. Create a free account
3. Provide SSN or EIN and verify identity
4. No dealer license required — open to the public

### Bidding
1. Browse available vehicles by location, make, model, year
2. GSA Fleet offers three sale methods: **online auction**, **live
   auction** (in-person), and **fixed-price** (buy-it-now)
3. For online auctions: place bids before the closing time
4. For live auctions: attend in person with your registration
5. For fixed-price: click "Buy" if the price is acceptable

### Payment & Pickup
1. No auction fees, no registration fees
2. Payment by ACH, wire, or credit card
3. Pickup at the designated GSA fleet location
4. Bring ID and payment confirmation

## GovDeals (govdeals.com)

**What they sell**: State, local, and some federal surplus — vehicles,
equipment, IT, furniture, confiscated property. The largest government
auction platform (5,300+ government clients).

### Registration
1. Go to [govdeals.com](https://www.govdeals.com/)
2. Click "Register" and create a free account
3. Provide your name, address, and payment information
4. Some sellers require additional verification (e.g., business license
   for certain equipment)
5. No dealer license required for most sales

### Bidding
1. Browse by category, location, or keyword
2. Filter by "Asset Type" and "Location" to narrow results
3. Each listing shows the current bid, time remaining, and pickup location
4. Place your bid; the system auto-increments up to your max bid
5. Auctions close at a specified time; highest bid wins
6. Some listings have a "Buy Now" option

### Payment
1. Buyer's premium: **7.5–12.5%** (tiered based on sale price), added to
   the winning bid
2. Payment methods vary by seller — typically credit card, ACH, or wire
3. Payment due within 2–5 business days
4. Sales tax may apply depending on the seller's state

### Pickup
1. Pickup location and timeframe are specified in each listing
2. You must pick up within the stated period (typically 5–10 business days)
3. Late pickup may incur storage fees
4. You are responsible for loading and transport
5. Bring ID and payment confirmation

### Tips
- Download the GovDeals mobile app for push notifications
- Search "sold" items to see historical prices
- Check the pickup location before bidding — it may be far from you
- Read the listing's "Special Instructions" carefully — each seller sets
  their own rules

## Copart (copart.com)

**What they sell**: Salvage, repo, and impound vehicles. Insurance
total-loss vehicles, repossessions, and abandoned vehicles.

### Registration
1. Go to [copart.com](https://www.copart.com/)
2. Register for one of three membership tiers:
   - **Basic** (free): Limited access; can bid on some public sales
   - **Advanced** ($59/year + deposit): Broader access; can bid on most
     public sales
   - **Dealer**: Full access to all sales including dealer-only inventory;
     requires a valid dealer license
3. Provide your driver's license and payment information
4. Verify your identity

### Bidding
1. Search by make, model, year, location, or VIN
2. Filter by "Sale Type" (Insurance, Repo, Public, Dealer Only)
3. Each listing shows damage type, title brand, run/drive status, and
   photos
4. **Read the damage description and title brand carefully** — see
   `auction-constraints.md` for title brand implications
5. Some sales are **export-only** — the vehicle cannot be titled in the US
6. Place your bid; Copart uses a timed auction format with proxy bidding
7. Some sales have a "Buy It Now" option

### Payment
1. Buyer's premium varies by membership tier and sale type
2. Additional fees: gate fee, title processing fee, virtual bid fee
3. Payment by credit card (with fee), wire transfer, or cashier's check
4. Payment due within 2–3 business days

### Pickup
1. Pickup at the Copart yard specified in the listing
2. You must pick up within 3–5 business days (varies by location)
3. Late pickup incurs storage fees ($25–$75/day)
4. Bring ID, payment confirmation, and your Copart membership card
5. You must arrange towing for non-drivable vehicles

### Critical Warnings
- **Check the title brand** before bidding — salvage, rebuilt, and
  certificate of destruction titles have different implications (see
  `auction-constraints.md`)
- **Check for export-only restriction** — if flagged, you cannot title or
  register the vehicle in the US
- **Check smog compliance** if you're in California or another
  strict-emissions state — many salvage vehicles won't pass smog
- **Get a pre-purchase inspection** if possible — Copart yards allow
  on-site inspection during posted hours
- **Budget for repairs** — add estimated repair costs to your max bid

## AutoBidMaster (autobidmaster.com) — Copart Broker

**What they sell**: Copart inventory accessible to the public without a
dealer license. Salvage and clean-title vehicles, motorcycles, boats,
RVs, equipment.

### Registration
1. Go to [autobidmaster.com](https://www.autobidmaster.com/)
2. Choose a membership tier:
   - **Basic** ($35): Entry-level access
   - **Advanced** ($189/year): Broader access, lower fees
   - **Premium** ($349/year): Full access, lowest fees
3. Provide your driver's license and payment information
4. No dealer license required — AutoBidMaster is a registered broker
   and market maker with Copart

### Bidding
1. Search Copart inventory through the AutoBidMaster interface
2. Place preliminary bids before the live auction, or bid live during
   the scheduled auction time
3. The vehicle is purchased through AutoBidMaster (not directly from
   Copart), so AutoBidMaster's transaction fees apply on top of Copart's
   auction fees

### Payment & Pickup
1. Multiple fees: AutoBidMaster transaction fee + Copart auction fee +
   website processing fees
2. Payment by credit card (with fee), wire transfer, or cashier's check
3. **Cannot drive vehicle off the lot** — must use an approved
   transporter
4. Shipping available through AutoBidMaster's transport partners
5. In-state purchases may require AutoBidMaster Home Delivery

### Tips
- Compare the total cost (bid + all fees) against buying directly from
  Copart with a dealer license or dealer agent — the broker fees add up
- Check the vehicle's title brand and export-only status (see
  `auction-constraints.md`)
- The membership tier affects both access and fees — run the numbers
  before choosing

## SCA Auction (sca.auction) — IAAI Broker

**What they sell**: IAAI inventory accessible to the public without a
dealer license. 300,000+ salvage and clean-title vehicles.

### Registration
1. Go to [sca.auction](https://sca.auction/)
2. Choose a membership tier:
   - **Basic** (free): $299 or 9% transaction fee (whichever is greater)
   - **Premier** ($199/year): $250 or 5% (whichever is greater)
   - **Expert** ($549/year): $200 or 3% (whichever is greater)
3. Provide your driver's license and payment information
4. No dealer license required — SCA is a marketplace that facilitates
   access to IAA auctions through affiliated dealers/brokers

### Bidding
1. Search IAAI inventory through the SCA interface
2. Place preliminary bids, use Buy It Now, or bid live (Premier/Expert
   only)
3. Additional fees on top of the transaction fee: IAA buyer fees,
   broker fee ($100), service fee ($105), environmental fee ($15),
   mailing fee ($20)

### Payment & Pickup
1. Payment by wire transfer or credit card (with limits)
2. **Buyer cannot pick up the vehicle themselves** — must use a
   licensed and insured transportation provider
3. 2–5 business day pickup window; $50/day storage fee if late
4. SCA has branch locations (e.g., Los Angeles at
   sca.auction/locations/branch-ca-los-angeles-134) but bidding is
   fully online

### Tips
- The total fee stack (transaction fee + IAA fees + broker/service/env/
  mailing) can add $400–$600+ to the winning bid — factor this into
  your max bid
- Compare against AutoBidMaster (Copart broker) — fees and inventory
  differ
- Check the vehicle's title brand and export-only status (see
  `auction-constraints.md`)

## US Marshals Service Auctions

**What they sell**: Seized/forfeited property — real estate, vehicles,
vessels, aircraft, jewelry, art, crypto, domain names.

### How to Participate
The US Marshals Service doesn't run auctions directly — it contracts with
private auctioneers. The process depends on which contractor is running
the sale:

| Contractor | What they handle | How to participate |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Bid4Assets** | Online forfeited property | Register at bid4assets.com/storefront/usms |
| **Apple Auctioneering Co.** | Seized vehicle & vessel auctions | Register at appleauctioneeringco.com/usmarshals |
| **Apple Towing / RBEX** | Federally seized vehicles (weekly) | Register at appletowing.com/auctions (via HiBid) |
| **AJ Willner Auctions** | Seized vehicles (NJ-based) | Register at ajwillnerauctions.com |
| **TXAuction** | Coins, bullion, precious metals | Register at txauction.com/USM |
| **RealLook.com** | Real property | Browse listings on reallook.com or Realtor.com/Zillow/Redfin |
| **CWS Marketing** | Treasury/Customs real & general property | Register at cwsmarketing.com |

### General Process
1. Check [usmarshals.gov/what-we-do/asset-forfeiture](https://www.usmarshals.gov/what-we-do/asset-forfeiture)
   for upcoming sales
2. Identify the contractor running the sale
3. Register on the contractor's platform
4. Review the due diligence materials (inspection reports, title
   information, condition reports)
5. Bid per the contractor's process
6. If you win, pay and take possession per the contractor's instructions

### Tips
- US Marshals property is generally sold with insurable title (for real
  estate) — but always verify
- Vehicle auctions are run by Apple Auctioneering and Apple Towing —
  register on both to see all available inventory
- Real property is also listed on Realtor.com, Zillow, and Redfin through
  licensed brokers at fair market value

## County Tax Deed / Foreclosure Auctions

**What they sell**: Real estate sold for unpaid property taxes or
foreclosure.

### How to Participate
1. Search for `"<county> tax deed auction"` or `"<county> trustee sale"`
2. Many counties use **Bid4Assets** — register there first
3. Some counties run their own auctions at the courthouse
4. Register and submit any required deposit
5. Attend the auction (in person or online)
6. If you win, pay by cashier's check or wire transfer (often same day or
   within 24 hours)

### Critical Warnings
- **No inspection contingency** — you buy the property as-is, sight unseen
- **No financing** — cash or cashier's check only
- **Title risks** — surviving liens, redemption rights, occupants (see
  `auction-constraints.md`)
- **Get a title search before bidding**
- **Consult a real estate attorney** if you're new to tax deed sales
- **Budget for eviction** if the property is occupied ($3,000–$10,000+,
  2–6 months)

## Copart / IAAI with a Dealer Agent (Broker)

If the user doesn't have a dealer license but wants to buy from
dealer-only sales at Copart or IAAI, they can use a **dealer agent**
(also called a broker):

1. Find a licensed dealer who offers broker services (search "Copart
   broker" or "IAAI broker" + your city)
2. The dealer bids on your behalf using their license
3. The dealer charges a fee (typically $300–$800 per vehicle)
4. The vehicle is titled in the dealer's name, then reassigned to you
5. You pay: winning bid + buyer's premium + broker fee + title transfer
   fees

### Warnings
- Verify the dealer's license is active with your state DMV
- Get the arrangement in writing (fee, services, timeline)
- The dealer is the legal buyer — make sure you trust them
- Some states have restrictions on dealer reassignments

truckloads.

### Registration
1. Go to [bstock.com](https://bstock.com/)

   - EIN or tax ID

### Bidding

2. Each listing shows the manifest (when available), condition grade,

3. Place bids; timed auction format
4. Some auctions have a "Buy Now" option
