# Salary Negotiation

## The Salary Band

Every role at a company has a **salary band** — a range from minimum to
maximum compensation for that level. Your interview performance determines
where you fall within that band.

| Interview performance | Band placement | Negotiation room |
|----------------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Below average | Bottom of band | Little to none |
| Average to above-average | Middle of band | Limited — may not reach top |
| Exceptional | Top of band | Some — but capped by the band |

If you fall in the middle (average to above-average performance), you may
not be able to negotiate to the top of the band. The band is a hard
constraint; recruiters cannot exceed it without special approval.

## Sign-On Bonus as an Alternative

When the base salary is capped by the band, a **sign-on bonus** is often
the negotiable lever.

### The Ramp-Up-Time Justification

The reasoning that justifies a sign-on bonus:

> If you can hit the ground running, your ramp-up time is very short
> (2 weeks vs. 2–3 months for someone they have to onboard and teach).
> You are saving the company 2–3 months worth of money, which justifies
> the sign-on bonus.

This is the same logic as the [Value Multiplier Reframe](value-multiplier-reframe.md) —
your experience saves the company money, and the sign-on bonus is a way to
share that savings upfront.

### How to Frame It

> "Based on my experience with [specific relevant work], I can be fully
> productive within 2–3 weeks rather than the typical 2–3 month onboarding.
> That saves the team significant ramp-up cost. Could we structure a
> sign-on bonus to reflect that, given the base is at the top of the band?"

## What Is Negotiable

Work with your recruiter and ask what is negotiable and what is not. There
is typically some flexibility in:

| Component | Often negotiable? | Notes |
|-----------|------------------|-------|
| Base salary | Within band only | Capped by the band; exceptional performers may get top |
| Sign-on bonus | Yes | The most common lever when base is capped |
| Equity / RSUs | Sometimes | Vesting schedule and grant size may have room |
| Relocation | Yes, if relocating | Often a fixed amount, but can be negotiated |
| Start date | Yes | Flexibility on start date is rarely contested |
| PTO | Rarely | Most companies have fixed PTO policies |
| Title | Sometimes | May be negotiable if it doesn't change the level/band |

## The Salary Expectations Question

When a recruiter asks about your salary expectations, **it is not a trick
question**. It is genuine. They want to know that your expectations are in
alignment with what they are able to give. It is not about not giving you
money — it is about not wasting time if the gap is too large.

### Script 1: Deflect to their range

> "I have done some research on [Company] compensation and their
> philosophy. I would really love to know from you, based on the level of
> this position, what does an average offer look like for this role?"

### Script 2: Ask for the band directly

> "I did do my research on this position, but I don't know what the salary
> band is for this role. Can you share that with me?"

### Why These Scripts Work

- They show you've done research (signals preparation)
- They deflect without giving a number first (avoids anchoring too low or
  screening yourself out too high)
- They ask for the band (gives you the constraint to negotiate within)
- They are polite and collaborative (signals "safe pair of hands")

### What Not to Say

- **"I'm flexible"** — Signals that you don't know your value or are
  desperate. The recruiter may anchor low.
- **"I'm currently making $X"** — Anchors to your current salary, not your
  market value. Never disclose current compensation if you can avoid it.
- **"I want $X"** (with a specific number) — Risks screening yourself out
  if too high, or leaving money on the table if too low. Always ask for
  their range first.

## Hiring Managers Want to Pay You

If hiring managers really like you, they want to give you as much money as
possible because they know you are the perfect fit. They will put their
neck on the line to extend their budget to bring you in.

This means:
- **Strong interview performance is the best negotiation leverage** —
  more than any script or tactic
- **Don't be adversarial** — The recruiter and hiring manager are not your
  opponents; they are your advocates if they want you
- **Be collaborative** — Frame negotiation as "how do we make this work"
  not "give me more"
