# Ownership Verbs

## The First Word Rule

The **first word of every bullet point** defines your perceived seniority
level. Recruiters and hiring managers read bullet leads as a proxy for your
role in the work — were you the driver or the passenger?

A resume full of "helped" and "supported" reads as junior, regardless of your
actual title. A resume full of "led" and "drove" reads as senior, regardless
of your actual title.

## Verbs to Use (Ownership / Senior)

| Verb | Signal |
|------|--------|
| Led | Direct leadership of people or projects |
| Spearheaded | Initiative-taking; you started it |
| Drove | Cross-functional momentum; you pushed it forward |
| Launched | You shipped something new |
| Owned | End-to-end accountability |
| Built | You created something from scratch |
| Architected | You designed the system or strategy |
| Established | You created a process or practice that didn't exist |
| Directed | Formal authority over a function |
| Championed | You advocated and won adoption |
| Transformed | You changed something significantly |
| Scaled | You grew something |
| Restructured | You reorganized for better outcomes |
| Negotiated | You represented the company in a deal |
| Pioneered | You did something first (use sparingly — can signal age) |

## Verbs to Avoid (Participant / Junior)

| Verb | Why it hurts |
|------|-------------|
| Helped | Positions you as an assistant, not a driver |
| Supported | Same — you were a passenger |
| Worked on | Vague; doesn't communicate your role |
| Assisted | Explicitly junior |
| Participated in | Passive participant |
| Contributed to | Implies you were one of many |
| Was responsible for | Describes a job description, not an achievement |
| Handled | Transactional, not strategic |
| Did | Too informal for any resume |
| Made | Vague and informal |

## Context Matters

Ownership verbs are necessary but not sufficient. After the verb, provide
context: explain the *point* of the project and who it helped, not just the
task performed.

**Weak:**
> Led a team of 8 engineers.

**Strong:**
> Led an 8-engineer team that cut deployment time by 70%, enabling the
> platform org to ship 3x more features per quarter.

## Translating Internal Language to External Outcomes

Recruiters look for recognizable signals, not potential. They will not
"decode" your past to see how it fits their future. Translate internal
company jargon into business outcomes that any hiring manager can understand.

| Internal jargon | External outcome |
|----------------|-----------------|
| "Owned the PAR process for Studio Tech" | "Managed the $2M annual technology investment approval process for the Studio engineering org" |
| "Drove TWDC-wide adoption of the analytics platform" | "Drove adoption of the analytics platform across 5 business segments, enabling $1B in attributed revenue" |
| "Participated in OSS Committee" | "Guided Corporate, Parks, Broadcast, Music, and Studio teams toward AI-based techniques and systems" |

## The Balance Test

Every resume should show balance across three dimensions:

1. **Technical credibility** — You can do the work
2. **Business impact** — Your work moved a metric that matters
3. **Leadership** — You elevated the people around you

If you are too technical, you look like a builder without direction. If you
are too strategic, you seem unable to roll up your sleeves. Synthesize
strengths in a single line where possible:

> Built a $200M product line by translating research into platforms.
> Developed the Python tooling that saved $2M annually.
