# Section Selection

## Standard Sections (Always Include)

| Section | Purpose | Notes |
|---------|---------|-------|
| **Header** | Name, contact, location, portfolio/LinkedIn URL | City/state only; no full address |
| **Summary** | 2–3 line positioning statement | Not an objective — see below |
| **Experience** | Dated work history with accomplishments | The core of the resume |
| **Education** | Degree, institution, year (if recent) | Ordering depends on seniority |
| **Skills** | Technical or core competency list | Placement debated — see below |

These five sections form the **minimum viable resume**. Everything else is
optional and should be included only when it strengthens your case for the
target role.

## Optional Sections (Include Only If Relevant)

| Section | Include when |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Certifications** | Required or valued for the target role (e.g., PMP, AWS, CPA) |
| **Publications** | Academic, research, or thought-leadership roles |
| **Speaking** | Conference talks, panels — signals industry authority |
| **Awards** | Recognized industry or company awards (not participation trophies) |
| **Volunteer work** | Relevant leadership or skills; gap-filling; values alignment |
| **Projects** | Side projects, open-source contributions — strong for technical roles |
| **Patents** | Engineering, R&D, or IP-driven roles |

> The rule for optional sections: **include only if it reinforces the case for
> the specific target role.** A certifications section with irrelevant entries
> is noise; a publications section for a staff-engineer role at a product
> company may be a distraction.

For senior candidates, optional sections like speaking, publications, and
patents can form a **credibility section** — see
[credibility-section-for-senior-careers.md](credibility-section-for-senior-careers.md).

## What to Omit

| Omit | Why |
|------|-----|
| **Objective statement** | Outdated; tells the employer what you want, not what you deliver. See [fluff-and-buzzword-elimination.md](fluff-and-buzzword-elimination.md) |
| **References** | Recruiters assume references are available; wastes space |
| **"Available upon request"** | Pure filler — every candidate has references available |
| **Hobbies / interests** | Rarely relevant; introduces bias and disclosure risk. See [content-exclusion-and-disclosure-prevention.md](content-exclusion-and-disclosure-prevention.md) |

For a full treatment of what to exclude and why, see
[content-exclusion-and-disclosure-prevention.md](content-exclusion-and-disclosure-prevention.md).

## Section Ordering by Seniority

### Experienced candidates (5+ years)

```
Header → Summary → Experience → Skills → Education → [Optional]
```

**Experience comes before education.** At this stage, your work track record
is far more persuasive than your degree. Education moves to the bottom and can
be compressed to a single line.

### New graduates and early career (0–5 years)

```
Header → Education → Skills → Experience → [Optional]
```

**Education comes first.** Your degree, relevant coursework, and academic
projects are your strongest evidence when work history is thin. Internships
and co-ops go under Experience but benefit from the education-first ordering.

### Career changers

```
Header → Summary → Skills → Experience → Education → [Optional]
```

Lead with a **reframing summary** and a **skills section** that highlights
transferable competencies, then follow with chronological experience. This is
the hybrid approach described in
[resume-format-selection.md](resume-format-selection.md).

## The Skills Section Debate

There is no universal rule for where the skills section goes or how detailed
it should be. The guidance depends on role type and seniority:

| Situation | Skills section guidance |
|-----------|-------------------------|
| **Technical / engineering roles** | Include a dedicated skills section near the top (after summary). List technologies, tools, and frameworks. Keep it scannable — group by category if long. |
| **Non-technical roles** | Place skills lower or fold them into the summary. A standalone skills block of soft skills ("leadership, communication") reads as fluff. |
| **Senior / executive roles** | A raw skills list is less valuable than demonstrated outcomes. Consider omitting the section or keeping it to hard, verifiable credentials (certifications, languages, platforms). |
| **Career changers** | Foreground transferable skills in a grouped skills summary, then back it up with experience bullets. |

### How detailed should it be?

- **Too sparse** (3–5 items): looks thin; misses keyword opportunities.
- **Sweet spot** (8–15 items grouped by category): scannable and keyword-rich.
- **Too dense** (25+ items): becomes a wall of text; recruiters skip it.

Avoid listing skills you cannot speak to in an interview. Every item in your
skills section is fair game for a probing question.
