# Networking and Relationship Building

## Build the Network Before You Need It

The worst time to build a network is when you are unemployed and desperate.
The best time is **years before** — when you can give help without needing
anything in return. A network built under pressure is transactional; a
network built over time is relational.

### What "Building" Looks Like

- **Stay in touch** with former colleagues — a comment on a promotion post,
  a quick "saw your team shipped X, congrats" message.
- **Help when asked** — introductions, references, advice. This is the
  deposit you withdraw later.
- **Be visible** — a complete, active LinkedIn profile (see [LinkedIn Profile
  Optimization](linkedin-profile-optimization.md)) makes you findable.

## The Weak Ties Principle

Mark Granovetter's research found that **most jobs come through weak ties** —
acquaintances, not close friends. Your close friends know what you know and
run in your circles. Your acquaintances bridge you to networks you don't
inhabit.

| Tie Type | Example | Job Lead Quality |
|----------|---------|------------------|
| **Strong tie** | Current teammate, best friend | Low — overlapping information |
| **Weak tie** | Former colleague, conference acquaintance | High — bridges to new networks |
| **Dormant tie** | Someone you worked with 3 years ago | High — reactivation unlocks latent access |

### Implication for the Search

Do not just reach out to your inner circle. Reach **one ring out** — to
people you worked with years ago, met at a conference, or connected with on
LinkedIn and never spoke to again.

## Informational Interviews

An informational interview is a **conversation to learn**, not a pitch for
a job. It is the highest-leverage networking activity because it is low-pressure
for both sides.

### The "No Ask" Rule

**Do not ask for a job.** Ask for advice and connections. The moment you ask
for a job, the other person feels pressure and their guard goes up. When you
ask for advice, they feel flattered and open up.

### How to Request One

| Step | What to Do |
|------|-----------|
| 1. Context | Name how you found them or what you share (alumni, former coworker, mutual connection) |
| 2. Specific ask | "Would you be open to a 20-minute chat about your experience at [company]?" |
| 3. No pressure | "I'm exploring [area] and would value your perspective — no expectation of anything beyond advice" |
| 4. Make it easy | Offer specific times, or let them pick |

### What to Ask

- **"How did you get into [field/team]?"** — people love telling their story.
- **"What does the day-to-day actually look like?"** — ground truth, not job
  description.
- **"What's the biggest challenge your team is facing?"** — reveals where
  you could add value.
- **"Is there anyone else you'd suggest I talk to?"** — this is the real
  ask: expanding your weak-tie network.

## The "Give Before You Ask" Principle

Before you ask anyone for a referral, introduction, or favor, ask yourself:
**what have I given this person?**

| Given | Asking For | Appropriate? |
|-------|-----------|--------------|
| Nothing | A referral | No — transactional |
| Helped them previously | A referral | Yes — relational |
| Shared useful info, commented thoughtfully | An introduction | Yes — you've earned it |
| Just met them | A job | No — too fast |

This is not a rigid ledger. It is a **posture** — lead with value, and the
asks become natural.

## LinkedIn Networking

A bare connection request ("I'd like to add you to my professional network")
is low-signal. Always include **context**.

### Good vs Bad Connection Requests

| Bad (bare) | Good (contextual) |
|------------|-------------------|
| "I'd like to connect." | "Hi Jane — I saw your talk at Kafka Summit on exactly-once delivery. I'm working through similar challenges on our payments pipeline and would love to connect and follow your work." |
| (no note) | "Hi Sam — we were both on the Acme payments team (overlapping 2022–2023, though different squads). Would be great to reconnect." |

### LinkedIn Networking Rules

- **Personalize every request** on desktop (the mobile app often strips notes).
- **Reference something specific** — a post, a shared employer, a talk.
- **Don't pitch in the first message.** Connect first, build rapport, then
  ask for a conversation.

## Where to Build the Network

| Channel | Best For |
|---------|----------|
| **Alumni networks** | Warm intros — shared school creates instant trust |
| **Professional associations** | Field-specific depth (ACM, IEEE, SRE groups) |
| **Meetups** | Local, low-pressure, recurring — shows up repeatedly |
| **Conferences** | Higher-signal but one-shot; follow up within 48 hours or the connection fades |
| **Former colleagues** | Dormant ties — the highest-ROI reactivation channel |

## Dormant Tie Reactivation

A **dormant tie** is someone you had a real relationship with but haven't
spoken to in 1–5 years. Reactivating these is often easier than building new
connections — the trust already exists, it just needs waking up.

### The Reactivation Message

| Step | Example |
|------|---------|
| Anchor in shared history | "Hi Alex — it's been a while since the Acme days. Hope you're doing well." |
| Genuine update ask | "I'd love to hear what you're up to now — coffee or a quick call sometime?" |
| No agenda stated | Do not mention job searching in the first message |

## How to Ask for a Referral

A referral is the single highest-conversion path into a company. But there
is a **right way and a wrong way** to ask.

### The Wrong Way

- "Can you refer me for this job?" (cold, no context, puts them on the spot)
- Asking someone you haven't spoken to in 3 years, with no warm-up.
- Asking before you've even tailored your resume for the role.

### The Right Way

| Step | What to Do |
|------|-----------|
| 1. Identify a role you're genuinely qualified for | Don't ask them to vouch for you if you're a stretch |
| 2. Warm the relationship first | A real conversation before the ask |
| 3. Make it easy | Send them the job link, your tailored resume, and a 2-sentence summary of why you fit |
| 4. Give them an out | "No pressure at all if you're not comfortable referring — I completely understand" |
| 5. Thank them regardless | Whether they refer or not, the relationship continues |

### Why the Right Way Works

A referral puts **their reputation on the line**. Make it low-risk for them
by being clearly qualified, prepared, and gracious about a "no."
