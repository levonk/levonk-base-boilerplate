# Background Check Preparation

## What to Expect

A **background check** is a standard part of the hiring process, typically
initiated after a conditional offer is extended. The scope varies by role,
industry, and jurisdiction, but most checks cover the following areas:

| Check type | What it covers | When it is run |
|------------|---------------|----------------|
| **Criminal background** | Felonies and misdemeanors on record | Standard for most roles |
| **Employment verification** | Dates of employment, job titles, sometimes salary | Standard for most roles |
| **Education verification** | Degrees earned, institutions attended, dates | Standard for most roles |
| **Credit check** | Credit history, debt, bankruptcies | Financial roles, executive roles, security clearances |
| **Reference checks** | Professional references contacted by phone or email | Standard, often separate from the background check |
| **Social media screening** | Public social media profiles reviewed for red flags | Increasingly common; sometimes automated |
| **Drug test** | Urine, hair, or saliva test for substances | Where legal and role-relevant (e.g., transportation, safety) |
| **Motor vehicle record** | Driving history, DUIs, license status | Roles involving driving |

Not every role requires every check. A software engineer will rarely face a
credit check or drug test; a financial analyst will almost always face a
credit check.

## What Can Disqualify You

| Disqualifier | Severity | Notes |
|--------------|----------|-------|
| **Discrepancies in dates or titles** | High — often treated as dishonesty | The most common cause of revoked offers. Even small discrepancies raise trust questions. |
| **Undisclosed criminal history** | High | The issue is often the non-disclosure, not the record itself. |
| **Falsified education** | High — instant disqualification | Claiming a degree you did not earn is fraud. |
| **Failed drug test** | High (where legal to test) | In jurisdictions where testing is legal, a failed test is usually disqualifying. |
| **Adverse credit history** | Medium — role-dependent | Relevant for financial roles; less relevant elsewhere. |
| **Negative reference** | Medium | A single negative reference may not disqualify, but a "would not rehire" from a recent manager is serious. |
| **Concerning social media** | Medium | Hate speech, illegal activity, or extreme unprofessional content. |

### The Honesty Threshold

The pattern across all disqualifiers is the same: **the cover-up is worse
than the crime.** A criminal record that is disclosed and explained is often
survivable. The same record discovered during the check — after you said
there was nothing — is treated as dishonesty and is almost always
disqualifying.

## How to Pre-empt Issues

### Be Honest About Gaps

Employment gaps are common and rarely disqualifying on their own. What
matters is whether the timeline you presented matches what the check finds.

| Gap reason | How to handle it |
|------------|-----------------|
| **Unemployment / job searching** | State it plainly. "Looking for the next role" is a complete answer. |
| **Health / medical** | You are not required to disclose specifics. "Medical leave" or "personal time" suffices. |
| **Caregiving** | "Caring for a family member" is a valid and respected reason. |
| **Travel / sabbatical** | State it. It is not a red flag. |
| **Education / upskilling** | State it. It shows initiative. |
| **Layoff** | State it. Layoffs are common and not a reflection on performance. |

### Disclose Before They Discover

If you know something will appear on your background check, **disclose it
before the check runs**. This applies to:

- Criminal history (even sealed or expunged records may surface)
- Credit issues (for roles requiring credit checks)
- Employment discrepancies you are aware of (e.g., a title that was
  informal but different from what HR has on file)
- Education nuances (e.g., a degree that is in progress, not completed)

### Explain Context

Disclosure is the first step. **Context** is the second. A bare "I have a
felony" is less effective than:

> "In [year], I was convicted of [offense]. I completed [sentence /
> program] in [year], and since then I have [specific evidence of
> rehabilitation — stable employment, community involvement, etc.]. I'm
> disclosing this proactively because I want you to hear it from me first."

Employers are often more concerned with **recency and pattern** than with
the existence of a record. A single old offense with evidence of
rehabilitation is very different from a recent pattern.

## The Timeline

| Phase | Duration |
|-------|----------|
| **Initiation** | 1–2 business days after you sign the authorization |
| **Criminal and employment verification** | 3–7 business days |
| **Education verification** | 3–10 business days (can be longer for international institutions) |
| **Credit check** | 1–3 business days |
| **Reference checks** | 3–7 business days (depends on reference responsiveness) |
| **Total (typical)** | **3–10 business days** |

Delays are common and usually not a signal. International checks, court
record backlogs, and unresponsive references can all extend the timeline. If
the check is taking longer than two weeks, a polite follow-up to the
recruiter is appropriate.

## Your Rights

### FCRA Disclosure

Under the **Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)** in the United States, you
have specific rights when a background check is run by a third-party
consumer reporting agency:

| Right | What it means |
|-------|--------------|
| **Written disclosure** | The employer must tell you in writing that a background check may be run, in a standalone document (not buried in an application). |
| **Written authorization** | You must provide written consent before the check is run. |
| **Pre-adverse action notice** | If the employer plans to take adverse action (e.g., revoking the offer) based on the report, they must give you a copy of the report and a notice of your rights *before* taking action. |
| **Adverse action notice** | If adverse action is taken, the employer must provide a formal notice including the name of the reporting agency and your right to dispute inaccuracies. |
| **Right to dispute** | You have the right to dispute inaccurate information in the report directly with the reporting agency. |

### What This Means in Practice

If an employer plans to revoke an offer based on a background check, they
cannot simply call and say "we found something, the offer is withdrawn."
They must:

1. Provide you with a copy of the background check report
2. Give you a notice of your rights
3. Wait a reasonable period (typically 5 business days) for you to respond
4. Only then take formal adverse action

This window is your opportunity to **dispute inaccuracies** or **provide
context**. If the report contains an error — a conviction that is not
yours, an employer that disputes dates you can prove — you can correct it
before the employer makes a final decision.

### State and Local Variations

Many states and cities have additional protections:

- **Ban-the-box laws** — Prohibit asking about criminal history on the
  initial application
- **Salary history bans** — Prohibit asking about or verifying prior salary
- **Credit check restrictions** — Some states limit credit checks to
  specific role types
- **Clean slate / expungement** — Some jurisdictions automatically seal
  certain records

Know the laws in your jurisdiction. If an employer violates them, you have
recourse.
