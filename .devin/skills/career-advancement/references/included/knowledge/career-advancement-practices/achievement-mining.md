# Achievement Mining

## The Blank Page Problem

When asked "what did you achieve at your last job?", most people draw a
blank. Not because they achieved nothing — but because **daily work feels
ordinary from the inside**. The fix is systematic extraction: you mine your
own history using structured techniques rather than relying on memory.

## Technique 1: Calendar Archaeology

Review the **past 12 months of your calendar**. Every meeting, project
kickoff, launch, and review is a potential achievement.

| Calendar Signal | Achievement Hidden Inside |
|-----------------|--------------------------|
| Recurring meeting you led | "Led weekly cross-team sync across 3 squads" |
| Project kickoff | "Drove initiative from scoping to delivery" |
| Launch / go-live | "Shipped X to production" |
| Post-mortem / incident review | "Reduced incident frequency after addressing root cause" |
| Demo / presentation | "Presented architecture to leadership" |

### How to Run It

1. Export or open your calendar for the last 12 months.
2. Scan month by month — note anything that involved **shipping, leading,
   fixing, or improving**.
3. For each item, ask: *what was the outcome?* (see Before/After framing
   below).

## Technique 2: Email and Project Management Search

Your inbox and task tracker are an **achievement archive**. Search for the
vocabulary of completion.

### Search Terms to Use

| Search Term | What It Surfaces |
|-------------|------------------|
| "shipped" | Releases and deliveries |
| "launched" | New features, products, or processes |
| "completed" | Finished projects and milestones |
| "deployed" | Infrastructure and code releases |
| "fixed" / "resolved" | Bugs, incidents, customer issues |
| "migrated" | Platform or data moves |
| "reduced" / "improved" | Optimization work |
| "thank you" / "great work" | Peer recognition — strong achievement signal |

### What to Do with Hits

For each hit, extract: **what was the situation, what did you do, what was
the result?** This is the raw material for resume bullets.

## Technique 3: Performance Reviews

Your manager's praise is a **pre-filtered achievement list**. They already
identified what mattered to the business.

| Review Signal | Achievement Translation |
|---------------|------------------------|
| "Exceeded expectations on X" | X is a headline achievement |
| "Promoted to / recognized for Y" | Y is a promotion-worthy bullet |
| "Took ownership of Z" | Z is an ownership story (see [Ownership Verbs](ownership-verbs.md)) |
| Praise from a skip-level or peer | Cross-functional impact — high value |

### If You Don't Have Reviews

Ask your manager informally: **"What were the things I did this year that
had the biggest impact from your perspective?"** Their answer is often
something you undervalued.

## Technique 4: The Before/After Framing

This is the **core structure** for turning raw work into an achievement.

| Element | Question | Example |
|---------|----------|---------|
| **Before** | What was broken, slow, missing, or expensive? | "Deployments took 45 minutes and failed 1 in 5" |
| **Action** | What did you do? | "Built a parallelized CI pipeline with automated rollback" |
| **After** | What was the measurable result? | "Cut deploy time to 6 minutes, reduced failures to near-zero" |

### Why This Works

The before/after structure forces **contrast** — which is what makes an
achievement legible to a reader who wasn't there. A bullet that says
"maintained the CI pipeline" is invisible. A bullet that says "cut deploy
time from 45 min to 6 min" is vivid (see [Metrics and
Quantification](metrics-and-quantification.md)).

## Technique 5: Ask Your Colleagues

You are the worst judge of your own impact because it feels routine to you.
**Ask the people you worked with.**

### Questions That Work

- "What did I help you with that saved you time or unblocked you?"
- "What's something I did that you'd mention if I asked you for a reference?"
- "What problem do you bring to me that you don't bring to others?"

Their answers surface **impact you've internalized and forgotten** —
especially the cross-functional, unglamorous work that doesn't feel like an
achievement from your seat but was critical from theirs.

## The Accomplishment Inventory

Keep a **running document** of every achievement, updated **quarterly** —
not just when you're job searching.

### Why Quarterly

- **Memory degrades.** An achievement captured 3 months later is vague; one
  captured the week it shipped is vivid.
- **Job searches are sudden.** When you need the inventory, you won't have
  time to reconstruct it from memory.
- **Performance reviews use it too.** The same inventory feeds your
  self-review, promotion case, and resume.

### Inventory Structure

| Field | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Date | 2026-Q2 |
| Project / context | Payments checkout reliability |
| Before | Checkout failures at 2.1% during peak |
| Action | Added retry with jitter, circuit breaker, queue backpressure |
| After | Failures dropped to 0.3%; recovered ~$X in abandoned carts |
| Category | Revenue / Quality |
| Verbs | Architected, implemented, reduced (see [Ownership Verbs](ownership-verbs.md)) |

## Categorizing Achievements

Every achievement falls into one or more **impact categories**. Categorizing
helps you pick the right bullet for the right job description.

| Category | Signal | Example Metric |
|----------|--------|----------------|
| **Revenue** | Directly or indirectly drove income | "+$1.2M ARR", "lifted conversion 15%" |
| **Cost** | Reduced spend or waste | "cut infra spend 30%", "saved 20 eng-hours/week" |
| **Time** | Speed, latency, cycle time | "deploy time 45min → 6min", "p95 latency halved" |
| **Quality** | Reliability, bugs, accuracy | "error rate 2.1% → 0.3%", "zero critical incidents in Q2" |
| **Scale** | Volume, users, throughput | "supported 10M req/day", "onboarded 50K users" |
| **Culture** | Team, mentorship, process | "mentored 4 engineers", "established on-call rotation" |

### Using Categories Strategically

Match the category to the **job description's priorities**. A revenue-focused
role wants revenue and scale bullets; a platform role wants time, quality,
and scale. This is the bridge to [Strategic
Abstraction](strategic-abstraction.md) — choosing which achievement to
elevate based on the audience.
