# Fluff and Buzzword Elimination

## The Problem with Fluff

Subjective claims and "empty words" decrease the perceived value of a
candidate. In the 125,484-resume study, over half of analyzed resumes were
weighed down by clichés that recruiters find annoying.

Fluff is any language that claims a quality without providing evidence. The
recruiter's reaction to fluff is not neutral — it is negative. "Team player"
doesn't just fail to help; it actively signals that you have nothing concrete
to offer.

## Typos: The Silent Offer Killer

A single typo can cause a hiring manager to reject an otherwise perfect
candidate. At Google, a hiring manager caught a typo that a recruiter had
missed and rejected the candidate on the spot:

> "This person doesn't pay attention to detail, I don't want to hire them."
> — A Google hiring manager, upon finding a typo in a perfect candidate's resume

### Why Typos Matter So Much

A lack of attention to detail can:
- Cost the company a lot of money (billing errors, deployment mistakes)
- In some cases, cost people's lives (safety-critical systems)

Companies use attention to detail in the resume as a proxy for attention to
detail on the job. If your resume has typos, they assume your work will too.

### What to Check

- **Spelling**: Zero typos. Use a spellchecker AND read it aloud (spellcheckers
  miss homophones like "their/there").
- **Formatting**: Zero inconsistencies. Same font, same bullet style, same
  date format throughout.
- **Company names**: Get them right. Misspelling the company you're applying
  to is an instant rejection.
- **Numbers**: Verify every metric. A wrong number is worse than no number.

## Terms to Purge

### Buzzwords and Clichés

Remove these terms entirely:

- "Team player"
- "Detail-oriented"
- "Results-driven"
- "Synergy" / "Synergies"
- "Passionate" / "Passions"
- "Creative mindset"
- "Scrappy"
- "Hard worker"
- "Think outside the box"
- "Go-getter"

### Personal Pronouns

Remove all personal pronouns: "I," "me," "we," "you." The use of pronouns can
hurt interview chances. Resume bullets should start with an action verb, not
a subject.

| With pronouns (bad) | Without pronouns (good) |
|---------------------|------------------------|
| I led a team of 12 engineers | Led a team of 12 engineers |
| We reduced costs by 30% | Reduced costs by 30% |

### Adverbs

Replace adverbs with evidence. Adverbs are subjective intensifiers; evidence
is objective proof.

| With adverbs (bad) | With evidence (good) |
|---------------------|---------------------|
| Successfully implemented the new system | Implemented the new system, achieving 95% daily usage within 30 days |
| Effectively managed the migration | Managed the migration with zero downtime across 40 services |
| Greatly improved performance | Improved query latency from 800ms to 200ms (75% reduction) |

### "Team-Oriented" Words

Words like "collaborated" or "assisted" can weaken a resume because they
position you as a participant, not a driver. Prioritize leadership verbs.

| Participant language | Driver language |
|----------------------|-----------------|
| Collaborated on the API redesign | Led the API redesign with a 3-engineer team |
| Assisted with the migration | Drove the migration of 40 services to the new platform |
| Supported the data team | Enabled the data team to ship 3 new pipelines by building the ingestion framework |

## The Replacement Principle

For every piece of fluff you remove, replace it with **evidence** — a specific
example, a metric, or a concrete outcome.

| Fluff claim | Evidence replacement |
|-------------|---------------------|
| "Entrepreneurial" | "Brought X product from concept to launch with a 3-person team and $50K budget" |
| "Detail-oriented" | "Caught a billing logic error that would have cost $200K/quarter; implemented automated reconciliation" |
| "Strong communicator" | "Presented quarterly architecture reviews to 40+ engineers and 5 VP-level stakeholders" |

## Audit Method

1. Search the resume for each buzzword in the purge list above
2. For each hit, ask: "What evidence proves this quality?"
3. Replace the buzzword with the evidence
4. If you cannot find evidence, the claim is unsupported — remove it
5. **Google search audit**: Search for "resume clichés and buzzwords" on
   Google and proactively delete any matches from your document. This
   catches terms you might not have on your own purge list.

## The Objective Statement Debate

There is no consensus on objective statements. Two credible sources
disagree:

**Against** (mainstream view): Objective statements are outdated fluff.
"Seeking a challenging role in a dynamic organization" tells the recruiter
nothing they don't already know. Most modern advice says to remove them.

**For** (contrarian view, Don Georgiovich): An objective statement clarifies
your intent and makes the recruiter's job easier — *if* it is exactly one
line and specifies the exact position and target industry:

> Senior Product Manager — Fintech

This is not fluff; it is a one-line positioning statement that tells the
recruiter what role you're targeting before they scan your experience.

### How to Decide

- **Use a one-line objective** if you are changing industries or your
  current title doesn't match the role you're targeting (see [Title Gap
  Bridging](title-gap-bridging.md))
- **Skip it** if your most recent title and experience already make the
  target role obvious
- **Never use a multi-line objective** — if it's more than one line, it's
  fluff
