# Application Strategy

## The Quality-Over-Volume Principle

Apply to jobs in a **targeted manner** — only a few per week — rather than
mass-applying to hundreds. Mass application is a numbers game that treats
every application as interchangeable; targeted application treats each
application as a campaign.

### Why Targeted Wins

- **Tailored resumes perform better.** Each application gets a resume
  tweaked for that specific job description (see [Keyword
  Engineering](keyword-engineering.md)). Mass-applying makes this
  impossible — you end up sending the same generic resume everywhere.
- **Recruiters can tell.** A generic application signals low interest. A
  tailored application signals genuine interest in *this* role, not just
  *any* role.
- **Energy management.** Job searching is exhausting. Spreading energy
  across 100 applications means 100 mediocre applications. Concentrating
  energy on 5 applications means 5 strong ones.

### The Targeted Application Workflow

1. **Identify 3–5 target roles per week.** Choose roles where you meet at
   least 70% of the requirements (see [ATS Reality vs
   Myths](ats-reality-vs-myths.md) for the OFCCP 100% rule exception).
2. **Tailor the resume for each role.** Adjust the summary, keywords, and
   bullet emphasis to match the job description.
3. **Write a personalized cover letter** (see below).
4. **Apply early** — within the first 48 hours of the posting if possible
   (see [ATS Reality vs Myths](ats-reality-vs-myths.md) — Apply Early).
5. **Track each application** — company, role, date, resume version sent,
   follow-up status.

## The Cover Letter Question

Create a **personalized cover letter for every targeted application**. The
cover letter is not a restatement of your resume — it is the bridge between
your experience and the specific problem the company is hiring you to solve.

### What a Cover Letter Should Do

1. **Name the specific problem.** Show you understand what the team is
   hiring for — not just the job title, but the underlying need.
2. **Connect 2–3 of your achievements to that problem.** Pick the
   achievements most relevant to *this* role, not your most impressive
   achievements overall.
3. **Show cultural awareness.** Reference something specific about the
   company — a recent product launch, a blog post, a public value — that
   shows you've done your homework (see [Interview
   Strategy](interview-strategy.md) — Cultural Fit).

### What a Cover Letter Should NOT Do

- **Restate your resume.** If it just lists your jobs and bullets, it's
  redundant.
- **Be longer than one page.** Half a page is ideal.
- **Use a generic template.** "Dear Hiring Manager, I am writing to express
  my interest in…" is the kiss of death.
