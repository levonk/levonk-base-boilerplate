# LinkedIn Profile Optimization

## The LinkedIn-Resume Relationship

LinkedIn is **not** a resume paste. It is a **living, searchable professional
identity** with its own optimization rules. Including a LinkedIn profile link
on your resume is highly beneficial — but only if the profile is high quality.
A **bare-bones or basic profile actually decreases** your callback rate. If
you include a LinkedIn link, the profile must be complete and professional.

## Headline Strategy

The headline is the single most important field for LinkedIn search ranking.

- **Never** use the default "[Title] at [Company]" format
- **Formula**: Role Descriptor + Transformation Angle + Key Credential
- **Example**: *Technology Transformation Executive | AI/ML & Cloud
  Modernization | Built $40M+ Data Platforms*
- **Length**: Under 220 characters (mobile truncation happens at ~120)
- **Keyword load**: Front-load terms recruiters search for (CTO, VP
  Engineering, AI/ML, Cloud, Data Platform)

The headline must echo the resume summary positioning (see [LinkedIn-Resume
Consistency Rules](#linkedin-resume-consistency-rules) below).

## About / Summary Section

The About section is where recruiters and hiring managers go after the
headline catches their attention.

- **First 300 characters** must stand alone — they display before "...see
  more"
- **Structure** (5 parts):
  1. **Hook** — transformation thesis in 1 sentence
  2. **Credibility anchor** — scale, scope, brands
  3. **Differentiator** — what you do that others don't
  4. **Current focus** — what you're building now
  5. **Call to action** — how to reach you / what roles you consider
- **Tone**: Conversational but authoritative — not resume-stiff, not
  social-media-casual
- **Length**: 800–1,500 characters (not words) — dense but scannable
- **No bullet points** — use short paragraphs and line breaks for visual
  rhythm
- **Include**: Links to public talks, articles, or portfolios if available

## Experience Entries

LinkedIn rewards narrative density, not bullet lists.

- **Do not** copy-paste resume bullets verbatim — LinkedIn scans dense
  narrative as higher quality than bullet dumps
- **For each role**:
  - **Opening sentence**: Scope + mandate + stakeholder level
  - **2–3 narrative paragraphs max**, each with a clear transformation story
  - **Embed metrics naturally** ("reduced cloud spend by 34%" not "Reduced
    cloud spend by 34%")
  - **End with what you built or enabled** that outlasted your tenure
- **Media attachments**: Add slide decks, project screenshots, or portfolio
  links to recent roles
- **Current role**: Always has the most detail; older roles compress
  progressively

## Skills & Endorsements

- **Top 3 pinned skills** must align with headline and target roles
- **Skill selection**: Prioritize search volume over precision
  - ✅ "Machine Learning" (high search volume)
  - ❌ "PyTorch Distributed Training" (too narrow)
- **Minimum 5 skills** that appear in target job descriptions
- **Remove** legacy skills that attract wrong opportunities ("Visual Basic",
  "Perl", "EJB") — see [Age Bias Coded Language](age-bias-coded-language.md)

## Featured Section

The Featured section is the highest-visibility content on the profile (above
the fold for anyone scrolling).

- **Use this section** — an empty Featured section is a missed opportunity
- **Recommended items**:
  - 1–2 articles or posts you've written about transformation/AI/data
  - A project deck or case study (PDF or SlideShare)
  - A link to a public talk or podcast appearance
  - A sales one-pager (if public-facing)
- **No**: Generic industry articles you didn't write, or anything with age
  signals

## Profile Elements That Matter

### Headshot

- Professional, current
- Face fills ~60% of the frame
- Neutral background
- No sunglasses, no group photos cropped to one person

### Banner

- Branded visual with a value proposition (not a stock photo)
- Example text: "Engineering Leadership | AI/ML | Cloud & Data Platforms"
- The banner is the highest-visibility real estate on the profile

### Connections

- Build meaningful connections — the study suggests a "comprehensive" profile
  involves active networking
- Quality over quantity, but a very low connection count signals inactivity

### Custom URL

- Use a custom LinkedIn URL (e.g., `linkedin.com/in/Levonk`, not
  `linkedin.com/in/john-smith-12345`)
- This keeps the resume looking clean and professional
- Add the LinkedIn URL to your resume header

## LinkedIn-Resume Consistency Rules

| Element | Consistency rule |
|---------|-----------------|
| Headline | Must echo resume summary positioning |
| Current role description | Must match the most recent entry in your resume |
| Metrics | Must be identical — no rounding discrepancies |
| Job titles | Must match or be recognizable variants ("VP, Engineering" vs "VP of Engineering" is fine; "Chief Wizard" is not) |

**If you update one, you must flag whether the other needs sync.**

## LinkedIn-Specific Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails |
|--------------|-------------|
| Pasting full resume bullets into Experience | LinkedIn scans as spam; poor readability |
| Using all caps in headline | Signals low sophistication |
| Listing every certification and course | Dilutes executive brand; signals insecurity |
| Endorsing everyone who endorses you | Reciprocity spam; devalues your network |
| Posting daily with no substance | Algorithm may deprioritize; weakens brand |
| Ignoring DMs from recruiters | Misses warm opportunities; reply even if not interested |
| "Seeking new opportunities" in headline | Weak positioning; use private settings instead |

## Profile Photo & Banner

### Photo

- Professional, current, face fills ~60% of frame, neutral background

### Banner

- Branded visual with value proposition text (not a stock photo)
- Example: "Engineering Leadership | AI/ML | Cloud & Data Platforms"

## Age-Neutrality on LinkedIn

LinkedIn displays full chronology, so age signals are harder to control than
on a resume:

- **Do not** list graduation years in the Education section
- **Do not** include first roles from the 1990s
- **Compress early history** — Merge pre-2012 roles into a single entry
  ("Prior roles at [Company], [Company]") or omit entirely
- **No "Open to Work" banner** for executive roles — signals desperation; use
  the private "Open to Opportunities" setting instead

## Activity & Content Strategy

### Posting Cadence

1–2 times per week minimum to stay in the feed algorithm.

### Content Mix

| Percentage | Content type | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| 40% | Insight/commentary on industry trends | AI regulation, cloud cost, data mesh |
| 30% | Behind-the-scenes leadership lessons | "What I learned scaling a team from 12 to 40" |
| 20% | Signal posts about current focus | "Currently architecting a real-time ML validation pipeline..." |
| 10% | Engagement with peers and target companies | Comments on target company posts |

**Never:** Complain, name internal conflicts, or share confidential project
details.

## Open-to-Opportunities Settings

- **Enable** "Open to work" privately to recruiters only
- **Target titles:** List the specific roles you are targeting
- **Locations:** Remote + specific target metros
- **Job types:** Full-time, Contract (for fractional inquiries)

## Connection & Outreach Strategy

- **Target connections:** Recruiters in target verticals, leaders at
  target-stage companies, board members, PE/VC partners
- **Connection request message:** Never generic. Reference a specific post,
  role, or mutual connection.
- **After connecting:** No immediate pitch. Engage with their content for 1–2
  weeks before any direct outreach.
