# Resume Format Selection

## The Three Core Formats

| Format | Structure | Best for |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Chronological** (reverse-chronological) | Work history in reverse date order, role by role | Steady career progression in one field |
| **Functional** (skills-based) | Skills grouped by competency, dates and employers minimized | Career changers, employment gaps |
| **Hybrid / Combination** | Skills summary up top, then chronological history below | Career changers who still want to show progression |

## When to Use Each

### Chronological — the default

This is the **standard format** and the one recruiters expect. Use it when:

- Your career shows **steady progression** in a single field or industry.
- Your most recent role is the strongest evidence for the target job.
- You have no significant employment gaps to explain.

Reverse-chronological ordering (most recent first) is the convention because
recruiters scan for recency — see [visual-scanning-patterns.md](visual-scanning-patterns.md).

### Functional — use with caution

Functional resumes lead with **skills and competencies** rather than a dated
work history. They are recommended for:

- **Career changers** whose transferable skills matter more than job titles.
- Candidates with **employment gaps** they want to de-emphasize.
- People returning to the workforce after an extended absence.

### Hybrid / Combination — best of both

The hybrid format opens with a **skills summary** (3–5 grouped competency
areas with bullets) and follows with a **full chronological work history**.
This lets career changers foreground transferable skills without hiding their
timeline. It is the safest choice when you need to reframe your experience
without triggering suspicion.

## Why Functional Resumes Trigger Suspicion

Recruiters and ATS systems **distrust functional resumes**. The reasoning is
simple: if you are hiding your timeline, you are hiding something. Common
assumptions when a recruiter sees a functional resume:

- **Employment gap** the candidate doesn't want to explain.
- **Job-hopping** disguised by grouping skills instead of showing roles.
- **Fabricated or inflated** experience that won't survive a date check.
- **Demotion or lateral move** masked as a skills narrative.

> A functional resume reads as a warning sign. The default assumption is that
> the candidate is omitting dates for a reason — and the reason is rarely
> flattering.

For a deeper treatment of how gaps and title changes are perceived, see
[title-gap-bridging.md](title-gap-bridging.md).

## The "Stealth Chronological" Technique

If you need a skills-forward narrative (career change, gap, reframe) but want
to avoid the functional-format stigma, use **stealth chronological**:

1. **Group bullets by competency** under each role (not a single skills block).
2. **Always include dates and company names** — never omit them.
3. **Lead each role with a skills-framed summary line**, then give dated
   accomplishments underneath.
4. **Keep the reverse-chronological order intact** so the timeline is
   scannable.

This gives you the reframing power of a functional resume while preserving the
transparency that recruiters and ATS systems expect. For ATS-specific
considerations, see [ats-reality-vs-myths.md](ats-reality-vs-myths.md).

### Example structure

```
Senior Engineer — Acme Corp (2022–Present)
  Leadership: Led team of 6; mentored 3 juniors to promotion.
  Architecture: Designed event-driven pipeline serving 12M events/day.
  Delivery: Cut deployment time 60% via CI/CD overhaul.
```

The competency labels reframe the work, but the dates and employer are
unmissable.

## Format Selection by Situation

| Situation | Recommended format | Why |
|-----------|--------------------|-----|
| Steady progression, same field | Chronological | Strongest evidence is recent roles |
| Career changer | Hybrid / stealth chronological | Reframe skills, keep timeline visible |
| Employment gap | Hybrid / stealth chronological | De-emphasize gap without hiding it |
| Recent grad | Chronological | Limited history; education + internships |
| Multiple short stints | Chronological | Don't disguise it — own the narrative |
| Executive / C-suite | Chronological | Functional is a red flag at this level |

## Executive Exception: Chronological Is Expected

For executive and C-suite candidates, **chronological is the only acceptable
format**. A functional resume at the executive level is an immediate red flag
because:

- Executives are hired on **track record and trajectory**, which requires a
  visible timeline.
- Boards and executive recruiters perform **deep reference checks** that map
  directly to dated roles.
- A functional format signals that the candidate is **obscuring a failure** —
  a demotion, a forced exit, or a gap — that will surface in due diligence
  anyway.

If you are an executive with a gap or a difficult transition, address it
honestly in a chronological format rather than hiding it in a functional one.
See [title-gap-bridging.md](title-gap-bridging.md) for framing strategies.
