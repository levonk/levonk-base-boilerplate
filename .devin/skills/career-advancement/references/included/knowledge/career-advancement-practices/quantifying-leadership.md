# Quantifying Leadership

## The Challenge

Leadership and soft-skill work is **harder to quantify** than revenue or
shipping velocity, but it is not unmeasurable. The mistake is reaching for
vague claims ("built a strong team culture") instead of finding the
observable signal underneath. Every leadership activity has a proxy metric —
you just have to name it.

For the general framework on quantification, see
[metrics-and-quantification.md](metrics-and-quantification.md). This page
covers the specific metrics that work for leadership work.

## Mentorship

| Metric | Example bullet |
|--------|----------------|
| Number of mentees promoted | "Mentored 4 engineers; 3 promoted to senior within 18 months." |
| Retention rate of mentored employees | "Mentored 6 new hires; 100% retention through first year (team avg: 78%)." |
| Time-to-productivity for new hires | "Cut new-hire ramp time from 12 weeks to 7 via structured onboarding." |
| Number of people you hired | "Grew team from 5 to 14 across 9 hires; 0 mis-hires." |

Mentorship bullets are strongest when they show **outcomes for the mentee**,
not just that mentoring happened.

## Culture-Building

Culture work is the hardest to quantify but has clear proxy metrics:

| Metric | Example bullet |
|--------|----------------|
| Employee engagement scores | "Raised team engagement score from 6.8 to 8.4 over two review cycles." |
| Retention rate before/after | "Reduced voluntary attrition from 22% to 8% in 18 months." |
| Team size growth | "Scaled org from 8 to 32 while maintaining engagement above 8.0." |
| Voluntary attrition reduction | "Held regrettable attrition at 0% for 3 years across 20-person org." |

### The "Before / After" Quantification

For culture work specifically, the **before/after frame** is the most
powerful pattern. It proves causation more than a single number can:

> "Inherited a team with 30% annual attrition and below-benchmark engagement
> (6.2). Within two years: attrition down to 9%, engagement at 8.3, and the
> team shipped 3 major releases on schedule."

Before/after works because it establishes the **baseline**, the
**intervention**, and the **result** in one breath.

## Engineering Excellence

For engineering leaders, excellence metrics are concrete and increasingly
standard (DORA metrics and similar):

| Metric | Example bullet |
|--------|----------------|
| Deployment frequency | "Increased deployment frequency from weekly to daily (3×/day at peak)." |
| MTTR (mean time to recover) | "Cut incident MTTR from 4 hours to 35 minutes via runbook automation." |
| Code review turnaround | "Reduced median review turnaround from 26 hours to 4 hours." |
| Incident reduction | "Dropped P0/P1 incidents from 11/quarter to 2/quarter over 12 months." |
| Test coverage | "Raised coverage from 42% to 88%; reduced production regressions 60%." |

These metrics are powerful because they are **verifiable and comparable**
across organizations.

## Organizational Design

| Metric | Example bullet |
|--------|----------------|
| Headcount managed | "Led org of 47 across 5 teams (engineering, data, platform)." |
| Budget controlled | "Owned $12M annual budget; delivered 8% under plan." |
| Span of control | "Direct reports: 6; extended org: 47; managed 3 layers down." |
| Layers in org | "Flattened structure from 4 layers to 2, improving decision speed." |
| Hiring throughput | "Built org from 12 to 47 in 14 months; 0 mis-hires." |

Org-design bullets establish the **scale and shape** of what you built, which
is the core evidence for senior and executive roles.

## Influence Without Authority

This is the leadership skill most often left unquantified. The signals:

| Metric | Example bullet |
|--------|----------------|
| Cross-team initiatives led | "Led 3-team initiative (eng, product, design) to ship unified onboarding." |
| Adoption rate of your proposals | "Drove adoption of internal RFC process across 4 teams (120+ engineers)." |
| Stakeholder satisfaction | "Maintained 4.6/5 stakeholder satisfaction across 8 product partners." |
| Decisions influenced | "Architecture proposal adopted as org standard by 6 teams." |
| Community / ecosystem impact | "Internal talk attended by 200+; spawned 2 follow-on working groups." |

Influence bullets work best when they show **scope of impact** (how many
people or teams adopted your work) rather than just that you "collaborated
well."

## The Leadership Quantification Framework

For any leadership bullet, ask:

1. **What changed?** (the outcome)
2. **What was it before?** (the baseline — for before/after framing)
3. **How many people / teams / dollars were involved?** (the scale)
4. **Over what time period?** (the timeline)

If you can answer all four, you have a quantified leadership bullet. If you
can only answer one or two, the claim is probably too vague to include.

## Choosing the Right Verb

Leadership bullets live or die on verb choice. Use **ownership verbs** that
signal agency — "led," "built," "drove," "scaled," "overhauled" — not passive
or participatory verbs like "helped," "supported," "participated in." See
[ownership-verbs.md](ownership-verbs.md) for the full verb ladder.

## Abstraction Level

For senior leaders, the right quantification often sits at a **higher level
of abstraction** than individual-contributor metrics. Don't list individual
ticket counts; lead with org-level outcomes and strategic impact. See
[strategic-abstraction.md](strategic-abstraction.md) for guidance on matching
abstraction to seniority.
