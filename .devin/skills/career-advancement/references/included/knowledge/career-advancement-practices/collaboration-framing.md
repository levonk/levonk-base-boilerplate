# Collaboration Framing

## The Core Principle

Collaboration bullets must lead with the **strategic impact** of the
collaboration, not the participation. The first question a reader asks is:
"What did you drive?" not "What committees were you on?"

A bullet that reads as attendance is noise. A bullet that reads as leadership
across boundaries is signal.

## The Three Collaboration Types

### 1. Cross-Functional / Cross-Segment Collaboration

Collaboration across teams, business units, or segments within the same
company.

**Fails (participation):**

> Participated in Open Source Software Committee.

**Passes (strategic impact):**

> Guided Corporate, Parks & Resorts, Broadcast, Music, and Studio teams
> toward AI-based techniques and systems.

The failing bullet says "I was on a committee." The passing bullet says "I
drove technology adoption across 5 business segments."

### 2. Vendor / External Partner Collaboration

Collaboration with external companies (cloud providers, technology vendors,
service partners).

**Fails (internal activity first):**

> Organized Studio Tech brown bags with Amazon to address S3 skew issues.

**Passes (collaboration first):**

> Collaborated with Amazon to refine S3 skew handling; organized Studio Tech
> brown bags to address the issue.

The failing bullet leads with the internal activity (organizing brown bags)
and buries the collaboration. The passing bullet leads with the collaboration
(Amazon) and positions the internal activity as the mechanism.

### 3. Cross-Organizational / Enterprise Collaboration

Collaboration that spans the entire enterprise or conglomerate.

**Fails (vague participation):**

> Engaged with leaders across the company on technology initiatives.

**Passes (specific scope + outcome):**

> Drove enterprise-wide adoption of the analytics platform across 5 business
> segments, enabling $1B in attributed revenue.

## The Framing Framework

### 1. Lead with what you drove

The verb should communicate ownership of the *outcome*, not attendance at the
*meeting* (see [Ownership Verbs](ownership-verbs.md)):

| Attendance verb | Impact verb |
|----------------|-------------|
| Participated in | Drove |
| Attended | Guided |
| Joined | Championed |
| Was a member of | Led |
| Engaged with | Collaborated with |

### 2. Name the parties specifically

"Across the company" is vague. "Across Corporate, Parks & Resorts, Broadcast,
Music, and Studio" is specific and communicates scope (see [Strategic
Abstraction](strategic-abstraction.md) — scope the impact).

### 3. Name the outcome

What changed because of the collaboration? If the bullet doesn't answer "so
what?", it fails the [Technology-Outcome Framing](technology-outcome-framing.md)
"so what" test — the same principle applies to collaboration.

## The Committee Trap

Committee membership is the most common collaboration framing failure. Being
on a committee is not an achievement — it is an assignment. The achievement is
what the committee accomplished under your influence.

| Committee membership (bad) | Committee impact (good) |
|----------------------------|------------------------|
| Member of the Technology Steering Committee | Influenced the $5M annual technology investment strategy as a member of the Technology Steering Committee |
| Sat on the Open Source Software Committee | Guided 5 business segments toward AI-based techniques and systems through the Open Source Software Committee |
| Participated in the Architecture Review Board | Drove adoption of cloud-native architecture standards across 12 services through the Architecture Review Board |

If the committee membership doesn't translate to a demonstrable impact, it
probably doesn't belong on the resume (see [Content Exclusion and Disclosure
Prevention](content-exclusion-and-disclosure-prevention.md)).

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- **[Ownership Verbs](ownership-verbs.md)** — Collaboration bullets still
  need ownership verbs. "Collaborated with" is acceptable; "participated in"
  is not.
- **[Strategic Abstraction](strategic-abstraction.md)** — Name the scope of
  collaboration specifically (teams, segments, business groups) to elevate
  altitude.
- **[Bullet Autonomy Principle](bullet-autonomy-principle.md)** — Each
  collaboration bullet must stand alone with action, object, and outcome.
- **[Technology-Outcome Framing](technology-outcome-framing.md)** — The "so
  what" test applies to collaboration: what changed because of it?
- **[Safe Pair of Hands Positioning](safe-pair-of-hands-positioning.md)** —
  Cross-boundary collaboration signals the ability to influence without
  authority, a key "safe pair of hands" trait.
