# Conciseness and Length

## The Word Count Sweet Spot

In the 125,484-resume study, resumes in the **475–600 word** range saw
**double the interview rate** of resumes outside that range.

Both extremes hurt:

| Word count | Problem |
|------------|---------|
| Under 475 | Looks thin; signals lack of substance or experience |
| 475–600 | Sweet spot — dense enough to prove substance, short enough to scan |
| 600–750 | Declining returns; recruiter fatigue sets in |
| Over 750 | 43% less likely to land an interview |

### Executive Exception

The 475–600 word range applies to typical and entry-level resumes (1–2 pages).
Executive, academic, federal, and C-suite resumes are an explicit exception —
they may appropriately exceed this range because the audience expects more
depth and the roles warrant more detail. However, even executive resumes
should not exceed 1,500 words, and every word must earn its place.

## Page Count Guidance

| Career stage | Pages | Rationale |
|-------------|-------|-----------|
| 0–5 years | 1 | Limited experience; one page is sufficient |
| 5–15 years | 1–2 | One page per 5–10 years of experience |
| 15+ years (non-executive) | 2 | Compress older roles; don't exceed 2 |
| Executive / C-suite | 2–3 | Full detail for recent roles; older roles summarized |
| Academic / Federal | 3+ | Exception: these formats have different conventions |

## The "3 Best Accomplishments" Rule

Do not try to tell your whole life story. For each role, highlight the **3
best accomplishments** that the hiring manager cannot miss.

> Your goal is not to tell your whole life story. It's to put your three best
> accomplishments for each role so the hiring manager can't miss them.

If a bullet doesn't reinforce what you are doing now for the specific job,
remove it.

## Weighting by Recency

Give recent roles more bullets and older roles fewer:

| Role recency | Bullets |
|-------------|---------|
| Current role | 4–5 bullets |
| 1–3 years ago | 3–4 bullets |
| 3–7 years ago | 2–3 bullets |
| 7+ years ago | 1–2 bullets (or omit entirely) |

## What to Remove

- **Mundane job duties** — "Changed paper in the copier" adds no value. Only
  include descriptions and accomplishments relevant to the target position.
- **"References available upon request"** — Outdated; wastes valuable space.
  Recruiters assume references are available.
- **Full street address** — City and state only. Saves space and protects
  privacy.
- **Outdated skills** — Remove skills that no longer add value (e.g., Windows
  95, Perl/CGI). They signal that you haven't kept current.
