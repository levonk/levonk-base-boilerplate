# Award and Recognition Framing

## The Problem

Internal company awards have names that are meaningful inside the company and
meaningless outside it. A recruiter or hiring manager at a different company
reads `"Cast Choice Award"` and thinks: "I have no idea what that is or why I
should care." The award becomes noise rather than signal.

## The Three-Part Rule

Every internal award or recognition reference must include three elements:

1. **The award name in quotation marks** (on first mention) — signals this is
   a named recognition, not a self-assessment
2. **A one-phrase explanation of what the award is** — the scope and criteria
   in language an outsider can understand
3. **A link to a business outcome** — why the award was given, grounded in a
   measurable result

### Example

**Good:**

> TWDC-wide "Cast Choice Award" for innovative technology — a
> cross-conglomerate recognition for the Marketing ROI project contributing to
> $1B in revenue.

**Bad:**

> Cast Choice Award for Marketing ROI project.

The bare form tells the reader nothing. A non-technical recruiter cannot
evaluate it. It reads as self-congratulation without evidence.

## Why Quotation Marks

Quotation marks on first mention signal that this is a **named award**, not a
self-assessment. Without quotes, `"Cast Choice Award"` could be the candidate
naming their own project. With quotes, it is clearly a formal recognition.

Subsequent mentions within the same document may drop the quotation marks.

## What to Explain

| Element | Why it matters to the reader |
|---------|------------------------------|
| **Scope** (team, org, company-wide, cross-company) | Tells the reader how significant the award is |
| **Criteria** (innovation, impact, leadership) | Tells the reader what the award recognizes |
| **Business outcome** (revenue, cost savings, adoption) | Grounds the award in evidence (see [Metrics and Quantification](metrics-and-quantification.md)) |

## External vs Internal Awards

### External Awards (industry, conference, community)

External awards are already recognizable to the target audience. They need
less framing — the awarding body's name provides the context:

> Speaker at PyData 2023 — presented on real-time ML validation pipelines.

Still link to a business outcome where possible, but the scope explanation is
less critical because the audience recognizes the awarding body.

### Internal Awards (company-specific)

Internal awards require the full three-part treatment. The awarding body is
the employer itself, which provides no external credibility signal. The
framing must substitute external context for the missing external recognition.

## What Not to Do

- **Do not** list awards without any explanation — a bare name is noise
- **Do not** inflate the scope — if it was a team award, say so
- **Do not** list participation certificates as awards — they signal
  insecurity at senior levels (see [Content Exclusion and Disclosure
  Prevention](content-exclusion-and-disclosure-prevention.md))
- **Do not** list expired certifications as awards — they are different things
  (see the candidate's style guide for expired certification conventions)
- **Do not** include age-signaling context ("this was done in 2012 when...")
