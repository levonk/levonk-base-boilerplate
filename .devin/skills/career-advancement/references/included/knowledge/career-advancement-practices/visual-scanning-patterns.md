# Visual Scanning Patterns

## The 7.4-Second Window

Recruiters spend an average of **7.4 seconds** per resume during the initial
scan. They do not read — they scan. The resume must be designed for scanning,
not for reading.

## Communicate Value, Not a To-Do List

Hiring managers and recruiters look for resumes that clearly communicate
your **value to the business**. A resume that reads like a to-do list —
"attended meetings," "managed inbox," "participated in sprint planning" —
tells the recruiter what you did, not what you achieved.

The distinction:

- **To-do list**: "Managed the team's sprint process and coordinated with
  product"
- **Value communication**: "Cut sprint cycle time 30% by restructuring
  the team's planning process"

Every bullet should answer: **what changed because you were there?**

## The F-Shaped Pattern

Eye-tracking studies show that recruiters scan resumes in an **F-shaped
pattern**:

1. **Top horizontal bar** — The recruiter reads across the top (summary,
   current title, most recent role).
2. **Left vertical bar** — The recruiter's eye tracks down the left side
   (company names, job titles, dates).
3. **Lower horizontal bars** — The recruiter scans across section starts and
   the first few bullets of each role.

The right side of the page and the bottom of sections receive significantly
less attention.

## Designing for the Scan

### Left-Align Critical Information

Put company names and job titles on the **left margin** — this is where the
recruiter's eye tracks during the vertical scan.

### Bold Company Names and Titles

Bold text draws the eye down the page during the initial scan. Bold company
names and job titles to create a visual "ladder" the recruiter follows.

### Provide Context for Unknown Companies

If your company is not a household name, add a one-line clarifier:

> **Tabby** — $1.5B Buy Now Pay Later platform

This anchors recognition for recruiters who don't know the company.

### Front-Load Bullet Points

Put the impact or metric within the **first 5–8 words** of each bullet.
Recruiters don't read full bullets — they read the first few words and decide
whether to continue.

| Back-loaded (bad) | Front-loaded (good) |
|-------------------|---------------------|
| By implementing a new caching layer, reduced API latency by 40% | Reduced API latency by 40% by implementing a new caching layer |
| Working with the marketing team, launched a campaign that increased CTR by 25% | Increased CTR by 25% launching a targeted campaign with the marketing team |

### Swap Bullet Order for Impact

Start with the accomplishment, not the method. "Improved Y 30% by doing Z"
stops the recruiter's scanning. "Did Z to improve Y by 30%" buries the lead.

## The Fold Test

**Diagnostic**: Fold the resume so only the **left third** shows. Would a
recruiter still be intrigued?

If the left third contains company names, titles, dates, and the first few
words of high-impact bullets, the resume passes the fold test. If the left
third is empty space or low-value content, the resume fails.

## First-Page Layout Strategy

Put all your best and most relevant information on the **first page**.
Ensure relevant experience begins **no more than one-third of the way down**
the first page. If a recruiter has to flip to page 2 to find your most
relevant role, you've buried the lead.

### Font and Readability

- Use a **10–11 point font** for body text — readable on both paper and
  screens
- Avoid fonts smaller than 10 pt (no 9 pt or 10.5 pt)
- Use a readable sans-serif font like **Arial** or Calibri
- Use professional, simple templates (e.g., Google Docs resume templates) —
  clean and boring beats creative and broken

## The 5-Second First Impression Audit

**Diagnostic**: Can a human understand your value if they skim the first
few lines in 5 seconds?

This is a stricter version of the fold test. While the fold test checks the
left third, the 5-second audit checks whether a recruiter who only reads
the top of page 1 — your summary, current title, and first bullet — would
understand what you do and why it matters.

If the top of page 1 doesn't communicate your value proposition, the
recruiter won't keep reading to find it.

## What to Avoid

- **Graphics, images, and columns** — These distract from content and can
  break ATS parsing. Use a "boring" template — clean, simple layouts perform
  better in eye-tracking studies.
- **Excessive colors** — Color distracts from the scan. Stick to black text
  with selective bolding.
- **Multi-column layouts** — ATS parsers read top-to-bottom, left-to-right.
  Multi-column layouts cause content to be read out of order or dropped.
- **Creative section headers** — "My Journey" instead of "Experience" confuses
  both parsers and recruiters. Use standard headers.
