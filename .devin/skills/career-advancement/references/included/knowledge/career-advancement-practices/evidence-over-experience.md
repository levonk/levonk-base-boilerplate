# Evidence Over Experience

## The Experience Paradox

Over 60% of entry-level roles now require prior professional experience.
Companies have outsourced training to the individual, creating a catch-22: you
need experience to get the job, but you need the job to get experience.

The solution is to shift from looking for "entry-level" labels to finding
roles built for potential, and to create **evidence** that replaces
traditional job titles.

> The issue isn't experience, it's evidence. And those are not the same thing.

## Alternative Job Titles to Target

Stop searching for "entry level" and search for these on-ramps:

| Title type | Examples | Why it works |
|-----------|----------|-------------|
| Associate / Junior | Junior Data Analyst, Marketing Associate | Often open to transferable skills |
| Trainee Programs | Customer Success Associate, Sales Dev Rep | Value learning agility over experience |
| Apprenticeships | Tech and finance apprenticeships | Paid work + structured learning |
| Rotational Programs | Finance, logistics rotational programs | Built to grow talent from within |
| Returnships | Re-entry programs for gap careers | Designed for non-traditional paths |
| Internships | Still the primary on-ramp for students | The most accepted "evidence" generator |
| Customer-facing entry | Support Rep, Service Coordinator | Internal launchpad into a company |

## The Evidence Framework

### 1. Strategic Volunteering

Volunteer in roles that **mirror the job functions you want**, not just any
volunteer work. Then write corporate-style bullet points for it.

| Volunteer task | Corporate bullet point |
|---------------|----------------------|
| "Managing non-profit events" | "Project Coordination: Managed logistics for 12 annual events serving 500+ attendees, coordinating 30 volunteers and a $15K budget" |
| "Updating the charity website" | "Web Development: Redesigned the nonprofit's WordPress site, improving page load time by 60% and increasing online donations by 25%" |

### 2. Personal Projects as Case Studies

Don't just list projects — turn them into case studies with documented
process and results.

**Case study structure:**
1. **Problem** — What issue were you trying to solve?
2. **Approach** — What did you build or do?
3. **Result** — What was the measurable outcome?
4. **Publication** — Share on LinkedIn or GitHub to attract recruiter attention

**Examples:**
- Marketing: Run an Instagram campaign and document the engagement metrics
- UX: Redesign an app's onboarding flow and document the before/after
- Data: Build a public dashboard from open data and document the insights

### 3. Micro-Credentials

Short-form certifications prove initiative and current skill relevance:
- Google Career Certificates (Data Analytics, UX Design, Project Management)
- Coursera specializations (2-hour courses to full specializations)
- AWS / Azure / GCP foundational certifications

**Share the learning, not just the badge.** Post about *what* you learned to
show a growth mindset, not just that you passed a test.

## Repositioning Non-Corporate Work

### Freelancing

Freelancing is often dismissed as "unstructured." Reposition it:

- **Frame as outcomes, not tasks** — "Increased conversion by X% for Client Y"
- **Highlight measurable impact** — Every project should have a metric
- **Leverage endorsements** — LinkedIn testimonials add external credibility
- **Maintain a portfolio** — If your work is visual, a portfolio is mandatory

### Targeting Startups

Startups care less about pedigree and more about proof of work:
- Value proof over pedigree
- A direct email with a project link is more effective than a cold resume
- Startups are more willing to hire based on demonstrated capability

## The Visibility Layer

Evidence only works if recruiters can see it:

- **Use LinkedIn as a "living resume"** — Document your progress publicly
- **Publish case studies on LinkedIn** — This can trigger recruiter DMs
- **Post before you feel "ready"** — Visibility compounds; start early
- **Send personalized emails with project links** when applying to smaller companies
- **Use LinkedIn endorsements and testimonials** for third-party credibility

## What This Is Not

This concept is for **entry-level candidates, career changers, and those
re-entering the workforce**. Senior executives with established track records
should not need to rely on personal projects or micro-credentials — their
proving ground is their recent professional experience (see [Proving Ground
Principle](proving-ground-principle.md)).
