# Recruiter Outreach

## Outbound Recruiter Outreach

[Recruiter Communication](recruiter-communication.md) covers **inbound** —
responding to recruiters who contact you. This document covers **outbound** —
you initiating contact with recruiters. Outbound is harder and less natural for
most candidates, but it is **the primary channel for senior roles, niche
specializations, and targeted company pursuit**.

## Who to Target

Not all recruiters are worth your outbound effort. **Targeting precision
matters more than volume.**

| Recruiter Type | Worth Outbound? | Why |
|----------------|----------------|-----|
| **In-house recruiters at target companies** | ✅ Yes — highest priority | They have direct roles; they are the decision pathway |
| **Specialized agency recruiters in your field** | ✅ Yes — high priority | They have niche roles you won't find on job boards; they know the market |
| **Generic agency recruiters (any industry)** | ❌ No | They spray your resume widely; low signal; often damage your reputation by misrepresenting you |
| **Retained executive search consultants** | ✅ Yes — for senior roles | They handle confidential searches; they are the gatekeeper for executive placements |

**Do not** waste effort on generic agency recruiters who recruit across all
industries. They add no signal and often submit you to roles without your
knowledge, creating duplicate submissions that disqualify you.

## The Outreach Message

The outreach message must be **specific, concise, and immediately actionable**.
Recruiters receive dozens of cold messages daily; generic ones are ignored.

### Message Structure

1. **Specific reference** — Name a specific role, team, or recent company
   development that prompted your outreach
2. **Fit explanation** — In 2–3 sentences, explain why you are a strong fit
   (relevant experience, measurable results, domain alignment)
3. **Resume attached** — Do not make them ask; attach or link it immediately
4. **Clear ask** — State what you want: a conversation about the role,
   consideration for the team, or information about the hiring process

### Message Example

> Hi [Name],
>
> I saw the [specific role] posting on [where you saw it] and wanted to reach
> out directly. I've spent the last 6 years building [relevant experience] —
> most recently [specific achievement with metrics] at [company]. The role
> aligns closely with my background in [domain].
>
> I've attached my resume. Would you be open to a brief conversation about the
> role and whether my background is a fit?
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

### Message Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails |
|--------------|-------------|
| Generic "I'm looking for opportunities" | No specificity; no reason to respond; indistinguishable from spam |
| Long career summary (5+ paragraphs) | Recruiters skim; they will not read a novel |
| No resume attached | Creates friction; recruiter moves to next message |
| Vague ask ("let's connect") | No clear action; no reason to prioritize you |
| Mass-sent identical message | Recruiters can tell; signals laziness and low interest |

## The LinkedIn Connection Request

**Always include a note** in your LinkedIn connection request to a recruiter.
A bare connection request with no context is the weakest possible outreach —
it signals low effort and low intent.

### Connection Note Structure

- **1–2 sentences max** (LinkedIn limits connection notes to 300 characters)
- **Reference something specific** — a role they posted, a company they
  recruit for, a mutual connection, or content they shared
- **State your purpose** — interested in a role, interested in their company,
  building your network in their space

| Approach | Example |
|----------|---------|
| Role-referenced | "Hi [Name] — I saw the [role] posting at [company]. I have [relevant background] and would love to connect." |
| Content-referenced | "Hi [Name] — really appreciated your post on [topic]. I work in [related area] and would value connecting." |
| Mutual-referenced | "Hi [Name] — [mutual connection] suggested I reach out. I'm exploring roles in [space] and would love to connect." |

**Never** send a bare connection request to a recruiter you want to work with.
The note is your first impression — make it count.

## The Follow-Up Cadence

Recruiters are busy and miss messages. **Polite, persistent follow-up is
expected and respected** — but there is a line between persistent and annoying.

| Touchpoint | Timing | Channel | Tone |
|-----------|--------|---------|------|
| Initial outreach | Day 0 | LinkedIn message or email | Specific, concise, resume attached |
| Follow-up 1 | Day 7 | Same channel | Brief: "Following up on my message — still interested in [role]" |
| Follow-up 2 | Day 21 (2 weeks after follow-up 1) | Same or alternate channel | Brief: "Wanted to circle back — happy to provide any additional info" |
| Stop | Day 35+ | — | No further follow-up; move on |

**After two follow-ups with no response, stop.** Continued messaging crosses
from persistent to intrusive. The recruiter is not interested or is too busy —
either way, your time is better spent elsewhere.

## The Relationship-Before-the-Ask Principle

The most effective recruiter outreach happens **before you need anything**.
Recruiters remember candidates who connected and engaged with no immediate ask
— when those candidates later reach out about a role, they are warm contacts,
not cold ones.

- **Connect before you need them** — Build your recruiter network during
  employment, not during job search
- **Engage with their content** — Like, comment on, and share recruiter posts
  that are relevant to your field
- **Send useful information** — If you see a role that fits someone in your
  network, send it to a recruiter; generosity is remembered
- **Be a good candidate when they do reach out** — Respond promptly, show up
  prepared, and close the loop even if you decline

| Timing | Activity | Result |
|--------|----------|--------|
| While employed | Connect, engage, share | Warm relationship established |
| When searching | Reach out about specific roles | Warm contact, not cold outreach |
| After placement | Stay in touch, refer others | Relationship compounds over career |

## When Outbound Beats Inbound

Outbound recruiter outreach is not always necessary — but in three scenarios,
it is **the most effective channel**:

| Scenario | Why Outbound Wins |
|----------|------------------|
| **Senior roles** | Senior and executive roles are often filled through targeted outreach, not job boards; the recruiter may not have posted the role publicly |
| **Niche specializations** | If your expertise is narrow (e.g., ML infrastructure, regulatory compliance in a specific domain), recruiters with that niche may not find you organically |
| **Targeting specific companies** | If you want to work at a named set of companies, contacting their in-house recruiters directly is faster than waiting for the right posting |

For junior-to-mid roles in common specializations, inbound and job-board
applications may suffice. For senior, niche, or targeted pursuit, **outbound is
the primary lever**.

## The Recruiter Network

Over the course of a career, build relationships with **5–10 recruiters** in
your field. This is not a one-time activity — it is a **compounding asset**.

- **Target mix**: 2–3 in-house recruiters at companies you admire, 2–3
  specialized agency recruiters in your domain, 1–2 retained search
  consultants for senior-level work
- **Maintain contact**: Touch base every 3–6 months, even when not searching —
  share market observations, refer strong candidates, update them on your
  career progress
- **Be reciprocal**: When a recruiter places you or helps you, refer them to
  others; reputation in the recruiter community compounds

| Network Size | Coverage | Effort |
|-------------|----------|--------|
| 0–2 recruiters | Low; dependent on inbound and job boards | Low |
| 3–5 recruiters | Moderate; most niches covered | Moderate (quarterly contact) |
| 5–10 recruiters | High; comprehensive market coverage | Moderate (distributed across quarters) |
| 10+ recruiters | Diminishing returns; hard to maintain quality | High; relationship quality degrades |

**5–10 quality relationships outperforms 50 shallow connections.** A recruiter
who knows your work and trusts you will surface opportunities that never reach
the public market.
