# Career Advancement Practices — Overview

This bundle synthesizes guidance for the **candidate side of the hiring
pipeline** — from resume writing through salary negotiation. Sources include
multiple recruiter perspectives (ex-Google, ex-Amazon, ex-Netflix
recruiters), eye-tracking studies (The Ladders), and a quantitative
analysis of 125,484 resumes (Austin Belcak).

The practices are general — they apply to any job seeker, regardless of
seniority or industry. Candidate-specific systems (like an AGENTS.md that
defines a specific person's resume hierarchy and guardrails) draw from
this bundle for the underlying evidence and reasoning.

## The Core Thesis

A resume is not a life story. It is a **signal** — a compressed document
designed to communicate to a stressed, time-constrained hiring manager that
you are a **safe pair of hands** who can solve their current problems
without creating new ones. Every writing decision should serve this thesis.

The same thesis extends to interviews and negotiation: the hiring manager
is looking for safety, not brilliance. Clarity, outcomes, and "done it
before" evidence win over cleverness and potential.

## The Hiring Pipeline (Candidate Perspective)

```
Job Description → Resume → ATS Parse → Recruiter Scan → Hiring Manager Read
     → Screening Call → Interviews → Offer → Negotiation → Accept
```

Each stage has different gatekeepers, different time constraints, and
different signals that pass or fail. This bundle covers every stage from
the candidate's side.

| Stage | Gatekeeper | Time budget | Key signal | Concept page |
|-------|-----------|-------------|-----------|--------------|
| Resume submission | ATS parser | 200–500ms | Machine-readable layout | [ATS Reality vs Myths](ats-reality-vs-myths.md) |
| Resume scan | Recruiter | 7.4 seconds | F-shaped scan, left third | [Visual Scanning Patterns](visual-scanning-patterns.md) |
| Resume read | Hiring manager | 2–4 minutes | Ownership, metrics, relevance | [Metrics and Quantification](metrics-and-quantification.md), [Ownership Verbs](ownership-verbs.md) |
| Screening call | Recruiter | 15–30 min | Pattern match, communication, salary fit | [Recruiter Communication](recruiter-communication.md), [Standard Interview Questions](standard-interview-questions.md) |
| Interviews | Hiring manager + peers | 45–60 min each | Team fit, gaps, cultural fit | [Interview Strategy](interview-strategy.md), [Standard Interview Questions](standard-interview-questions.md) |
| Offer & negotiation | Recruiter + hiring manager | Days | Band placement, sign-on leverage | [Salary Negotiation](salary-negotiation.md) |

## How the Concepts Fit Together

### Resume System Architecture (the system that manages your resumes)

- **[Resume Architecture and Lineage](resume-architecture-and-lineage.md)** —
  A multi-tier system (archive → working resume → tailored variants) with a
  root-first lineage rule. The archive is the complete career record; the
  working resume is derived from it; tailored variants are derived from the
  working resume. All changes start at the root and propagate downstream.
- **[Application Funnel Stages](application-funnel-stages.md)** — The S0-S5
  funnel with measurable conversion objectives at each stage. Different
  resume variants are optimized for different stages. Never optimize an
  early stage at the expense of a later stage.
- **[Job Description Tailoring Workflow](job-description-tailoring-
  workflow.md)** — The 5-step process for generating a tailored variant from
  the working resume: ingest JD, gap analysis, mutate, verify ATS
  compatibility, output package.
- **[Answer Sheet and Terminology Guide](answer-sheet-terminology-guide.md)**
  — A companion document explaining jargon, internal terms, and acronyms for
  reviewers. Not a resume artifact — reference material for recruiters,
  hiring managers, and AI agents.

### Resume Content (what you write)

- **[Metrics and Quantification](metrics-and-quantification.md)** — Numbers
  make you 40% more likely to get an interview. Use the X-Y-Z formula.
- **[Achievement Mining](achievement-mining.md)** — How to extract
  achievements from your work history when drawing a blank: calendar
  archaeology, email/PM search, performance reviews, the before/after
  framing, and the quarterly accomplishment inventory.
- **[Quantifying Leadership](quantifying-leadership.md)** — How to quantify
  hard-to-measure leadership and soft-skill work: mentorship, culture-
  building, engineering excellence, organizational design, and influence
  without authority.
- **[Ownership Verbs](ownership-verbs.md)** — The first word of every bullet
  defines your perceived seniority. Lead with "Led," not "Helped."
- **[Fluff and Buzzword Elimination](fluff-and-buzzword-elimination.md)** —
  Buzzwords and personal pronouns decrease perceived value. Replace with
  evidence.
- **[Keyword Engineering](keyword-engineering.md)** — Match job description
  terminology naturally. Don't stuff. Place keywords in specific zones.
- **[Strategic Abstraction](strategic-abstraction.md)** — Match the
  abstraction altitude of every bullet to the seniority of the target role.
  An IC-altitude bullet on an executive resume undersells the candidate.
  Generalize specific technology adoptions into patterns of organizational
  change.
- **[Technology-Outcome Framing](technology-outcome-framing.md)** — Frame
  every technology reference as a means to a business end, never as an end
  in itself. The Early-Adopter Trap — framing adoption as novelty-chasing —
  signals buzzword-driven development and recklessness rather than strategic
  judgment.
- **[Bullet Autonomy Principle](bullet-autonomy-principle.md)** — Every
  bullet must stand alone with a clear action, object, and outcome. Remove
  sub-point bullets that depend on another bullet for context. Recruiters
  read bullets out of order and in isolation.
- **[Award and Recognition Framing](award-and-recognition-framing.md)** —
  Internal company awards need three elements: the name in quotes, a
  one-phrase explanation of what it is, and a link to a business outcome.
  A bare award name is noise to an outside recruiter.
- **[Collaboration Framing](collaboration-framing.md)** — Lead with the
  strategic impact of collaboration, not the participation. "What did you
  drive?" not "what committees were you on?" Committee membership is an
  assignment, not an achievement.

### Resume Structure (how you organize it)

- **[Conciseness and Length](conciseness-and-length.md)** — There is a word
  count sweet spot. Going over or under hurts interview rates.
- **[Visual Scanning Patterns](visual-scanning-patterns.md)** — Recruiters
  scan in an F-shape. Design for the scan, not for aesthetics.
- **[Title Gap Bridging](title-gap-bridging.md)** — When your title doesn't
  match the target role, bridge the gap with parenthetical clarifiers.
- **[Credibility Section for Senior Careers](credibility-section-for-senior-
  careers.md)** — Condense pre-2012 or early-career roles into a single
  narrative paragraph with metrics but no dates or role titles. Retains the
  credibility signal without the age signal.
- **[Resume Format Selection](resume-format-selection.md)** — Chronological
  vs functional vs hybrid. Functional resumes trigger suspicion (hiding
  something). Chronological is expected for executives; functional is a red
  flag. The stealth chronological technique for career changers.
- **[Section Selection](section-selection.md)** — What sections to include
  and omit by seniority. Optional sections (certifications, publications,
  speaking, volunteer) only if relevant. Ordering: experience before
  education for experienced candidates; education first for new grads.
- **[Portfolio and Work Samples](portfolio-work-samples.md)** — GitHub repos,
  case studies, design and writing portfolios. The portfolio-as-proof
  principle — showing is stronger than telling. When a portfolio outweighs
  the resume (creative roles, early-career, career changers).

### Positioning (how you frame yourself — spans resume, interview, and negotiation)

- **[Safe Pair of Hands Positioning](safe-pair-of-hands-positioning.md)** —
  Managers hire safety over brilliance. Make your resume's primary job to look
  safe. Avoid the Builder Trap (too technical) and Fluff Strategy (too
  strategic) anti-patterns.
- **[Proving Ground Principle](proving-ground-principle.md)** — The last 5–7
  years are your primary value signal. Weight accordingly.
- **[Evidence Over Experience](evidence-over-experience.md)** — When you lack
  the title, provide proof: projects, volunteering, case studies.
- **[Application Strategy](application-strategy.md)** — Targeted applications
  outperform mass-applying. Personalize the cover letter for each role.
- **[Chronological Accuracy and Name Changes](chronological-accuracy-and-name-
  changes.md)** — Use time-appropriate names in the archive, modern names in
  current documents, and the recognizability exception when the modern name
  is obscure to the target audience.
- **[Career Transition](career-transition.md)** — How to pivot industries or
  functions: the bridge strategy, building credibility in the new domain,
  the narrative pivot, the transferable skills translation framework, and
  the stepping stone approach.

### Interview & Hiring Process (what happens after the resume passes)

- **[Interview Strategy](interview-strategy.md)** — Team composition
  determines hiring, the fast-track scenario, OE/OA metrics, cultural fit,

  desperation, the 5-minute decision, culture fit as bias shield, and
  asking the right questions.
- **[Standard Interview Questions](standard-interview-questions.md)** —
  Frameworks and generic fallback answers for the predictable questions
  every interview asks: the opening, the departure reason, the employment
  gap, the 5-year plan, the overqualified/underqualified challenge,
  management and communication style, and the two audience-aware questions
  ("what questions do you have for me?" and "what concerns do you have
  about this role?") that change by who is asking. Includes a full
  question index with cross-links. The preparation layer for the
  five-minute decision.
- **[Behavioral Interview Framework](behavioral-interview-framework.md)** —
  The STAR/PAR/CAR methods for structuring behavioral answers, the
  lead-with-result variant for resume bullets, the 7-theme story bank

  and common mistakes.
- **[Technical Interview Preparation](technical-interview-preparation.md)**
  — Coding interviews (live, whiteboard, IDE), system design (the 4-step

- **[Panel and Group Interviews](panel-group-interview.md)** — Handling

- **[Case Interviews](case-interview.md)** — Consulting-style case

- **[Presentation and Demo Interviews](presentation-demo-interview.md)** —

- **[Recruiter Communication](recruiter-communication.md)** — Recruiters as

- **[Interview Follow-Up](interview-follow-up.md)** — Thank you notes

- **[Reference Management](reference-management.md)** — Who to list as

## What This Bundle Is Not

- **Not** a collection of source references — each concept page contains
  actionable knowledge, not a bibliography
- **Not** specific to any individual candidate — these are general practices
- **Not** a replacement for candidate-specific system specifications (like an
  AGENTS.md that defines a specific candidate's resume hierarchy, guardrails,
  and workflow) — this bundle provides the underlying evidence and reasoning
  that those specifications draw from
- **Not** a recruiter-side or hiring-manager-side guide — this is the
  candidate's playbook
