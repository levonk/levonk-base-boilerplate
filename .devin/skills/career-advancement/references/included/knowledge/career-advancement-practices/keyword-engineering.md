# Keyword Engineering

## The Keyword Reality

Most candidates match only about **half** of the keywords in a target job
description. The average job description contains **43 keywords** — aim to
match as many as possible, naturally embedded in achievement bullets.

## The Soft-Skills Gap

Resumes typically under-index on **soft skills** (28% match rate) compared to
hard skills (60% match rate). This is a missed opportunity — soft skills are
heavily searched by recruiters and are often the differentiator between two
technically qualified candidates.

### In-Demand Soft Skills to Embed

- Communication (cross-functional, executive-level)
- Leadership (team building, mentoring, stakeholder management)
- Adaptability (pivoting strategy, responding to market shifts)
- Problem-solving (root-cause analysis, ambiguity navigation)
- Collaboration (cross-team, vendor, partner)
- Emotional intelligence (conflict resolution, stakeholder alignment)

### How to Embed Soft Skills

Do not list soft skills in a skills section — that is fluff. Instead, embed
them in achievement bullets as the *method*:

> Led cross-functional alignment across Engineering, Product, and Finance
> to prioritize the API redesign, reducing integration time by 40% for
> downstream teams.

The soft skill ("cross-functional alignment") is demonstrated through the
achievement, not claimed as a trait.

## Where to Place Keywords

### High-Value Zones

1. **Summary / Professional Profile** — The first section a recruiter reads.
   Embed 3–5 target keywords here.
2. **Skills section** — List hard skills that match the JD. Use the JD's exact
   terminology.
3. **Achievement bullets** — Embed keywords naturally in the method (the "Z"
   in X-Y-Z).
4. **Job titles** — If your title doesn't match the JD, add a parenthetical
   clarifier (see [Title Gap Bridging](title-gap-bridging.md)).

### Low-Value Zones (Avoid)

- **Objective statements** — Outdated; waste of keyword real estate
- **References section** — No one reads this
- **Hobbies / Interests** — Irrelevant for keyword matching

## Natural Embedding vs. Stuffing

Keyword stuffing — listing keywords in a block or forcing them into sentences
unnaturally — is detectable and signals desperation. Natural embedding means
the keyword appears because it describes what you actually did.

| Stuffed (bad) | Natural (good) |
|---------------|----------------|
| "Kubernetes, Docker, Terraform, AWS, GCP, Azure, CI/CD, microservices, distributed systems" | "Architected a Kubernetes-based platform on AWS supporting 40 microservices, deployed via Terraform-managed CI/CD pipelines" |
| "Stakeholder alignment, cross-functional collaboration, executive communication" | "Aligned 5 VP-level stakeholders on the platform consolidation strategy, reducing infrastructure spend by 30%" |

## The Word Cloud Method

1. Copy the target job description
2. Paste it into a word cloud tool (e.g., wordclouds.com)
3. Identify the largest terms — these are the most frequent keywords
4. Cross-reference against your resume
5. For each missing keyword, find a bullet where you can naturally embed it
6. If you cannot embed it naturally, you may not actually have that experience
   — don't fabricate it

## Keyword Mirroring

Recruiters look for recognizable signals, not potential. They will not
"decode" your past to see how it fits their future. **Mirror the job
description's exact verbiage** when describing your experience.

| JD says | Your resume should say | Don't say |
|---------|----------------------|-----------|
| "Stakeholder alignment" | "Aligned stakeholders across..." | "Got everyone on the same page" |
| "Cross-functional collaboration" | "Collaborated cross-functionally with..." | "Worked with other teams" |
| "Data-driven decision making" | "Drove data-driven decisions by..." | "Used data to make choices" |
