# Job Search Strategy

## The Job Search as a Funnel

A job search is not a single event — it is a **funnel** with measurable
conversion rates at each stage. Treating the search as a funnel lets you
diagnose where you are stuck instead of guessing.

### The Four Stages

| Stage | Input | Output | Typical Conversion |
|-------|-------|--------|--------------------|
| Applications | Your submitted applications | Phone screens | 10–20% |
| Phone screens | Recruiter or hiring-manager calls | Onsite interviews | 30–50% |
| Onsite interviews | Full interview loops | Offers | 20–40% |
| Offers | Received offers | Accepted offer | N/A (your choice) |

### Funnel Math in Practice

If you want **2 offers** on the table, work backward:

- 2 offers ← ~5–10 onsites (at 20–40% conversion)
- 5–10 onsites ← ~10–33 phone screens (at 30–50% conversion)
- 10–33 phone screens ← ~50–330 applications (at 10–20% conversion)

The wide range is why **pipeline volume matters** — you cannot control
conversion at any single stage, but you can control how much you feed the top.

## The 70-20-10 Time Allocation Rule

Where you spend your search hours matters more than how many hours you spend.

| Activity | Allocation | Why |
|----------|-----------|-----|
| **Active outreach & networking** | 70% | Highest-signal channel — referrals and warm intros convert far better than cold applications |
| **Targeted applications** | 20% | Direct applications to carefully chosen roles (see [Application Strategy](application-strategy.md)) |
| **Job boards** | 10% | Low signal but useful for discovery and market scanning (see [Job Board Strategy](job-board-strategy.md)) |

The common mistake is **inverting the ratio** — spending 90% on job boards and
10% (or zero) on networking. This produces a top-of-funnel full of
low-conversion applications.

## The Pipeline Concept

A healthy search has **multiple opportunities at different stages** at all
times. You should never be waiting on a single company.

| Pipeline State | Healthy | Stuck |
|---------------|---------|-------|
| Top of funnel | 5–10 active applications in flight | Zero new applications in 2+ weeks |
| Mid-funnel | 2–4 phone screens scheduled | No screens in 2+ weeks |
| Bottom of funnel | 1–2 onsites or offers | Nothing past phone screen in 3+ weeks |

### Why the Pipeline Matters

- **Leverage in negotiation.** Multiple offers let you negotiate from
  strength (see [Salary Negotiation](salary-negotiation.md)).
- **Emotional resilience.** A single rejection stings less when 5 other
  conversations are ongoing.
- **Time pressure resistance.** You are less likely to accept a bad offer
  out of desperation when the pipeline is full.

## Tracking Systems

You cannot manage a funnel you cannot see. Use a **spreadsheet or CRM** to
track every opportunity.

### Minimum Tracking Fields

| Field | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Company | Acme Corp |
| Role | Senior Backend Engineer |
| Date applied | 2026-08-01 |
| Contact | Jane Smith (recruiter) |
| Stage | Phone screen scheduled |
| Next step | Call on 2026-08-05 |
| Notes | Referred by Bob; team is the payments platform group |

### Why Tracking Matters

- **Follow-up discipline.** If you haven't heard back in 7 days, you follow
  up. Without tracking, you forget.
- **Stage diagnosis.** If 30 applications produced 1 phone screen, your
  problem is the resume or targeting — not the market.
- **Pipeline visibility.** A glance at the tracker tells you whether the
  funnel is full or emptying out.

## Weekly Cadence

A disciplined search runs on a **weekly rhythm**, not ad-hoc bursts.

| Activity | Weekly Target |
|----------|---------------|
| Networking conversations | 5–10 (informational interviews, reconnects) |
| Targeted applications | 3–5 (quality over volume) |
| Job board scanning | 1–2 sessions (for discovery, not bulk applying) |
| Follow-ups | All stale entries (7+ days no response) |

The cadence is sustainable. Burnout comes from sprinting (50 applications in
a week, then nothing for a month), not from steady effort.

## Signs Your Search Is Stuck

Diagnose by **where conversion drops** in the funnel.

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Many applications, few phone screens | Resume not passing ATS or not tailored | See [Keyword Engineering](keyword-engineering.md), [ATS Reality vs Myths](ats-reality-vs-myths.md) |
| Many phone screens, few onsites | Phone presence, framing, or targeting mismatch | See [Interview Strategy](interview-strategy.md) |
| Many onsites, few offers | Interview performance or fit signaling | See [Interview Strategy](interview-strategy.md), [Standard Interview Questions](standard-interview-questions.md) |
| Few applications at all | Pipeline empty — not enough outreach | Shift time toward the 70% networking bucket |

### The "Stuck at One Stage" Pattern

If conversion collapses at **one specific stage**, that is your bottleneck.
Fix that stage before adding more volume to the top — otherwise you are
pouring water into a leaky funnel.
