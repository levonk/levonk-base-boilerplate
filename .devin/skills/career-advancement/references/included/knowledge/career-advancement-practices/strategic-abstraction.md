# Strategic Abstraction

## The Altitude Principle

Every resume bullet operates at an **abstraction altitude** — the level of
specificity at which the work is described. There are four altitudes:

| Altitude | describes | Example |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| **Implementation** | A specific task or technology operation | "Wrote Python scripts to parse log files" |
| **Team** | What a team delivered under your guidance | "Led a 4-engineer team that shipped the ingestion pipeline" |
| **Org** | Cross-team or department-wide change | "Drove adoption of the analytics platform across 5 business segments" |
| **Enterprise** | Company-wide or conglomerate-wide transformation | "Catalyzed enterprise-wide adoption of cloud-native practices across 20+ business groups" |

The altitude of your bullets tells the reader what **level** you operate at.
A resume full of implementation-altitude bullets reads as an IC, regardless
of your title. A resume full of enterprise-altitude bullets reads as an
executive, regardless of your title.

## The Core Rule

**Match the bullet altitude to the seniority of the target role.**

If you are targeting an executive role (CTO, VP, Director), your bullets
should operate at **org or enterprise altitude**. If your resume is full of
implementation-altitude bullets, the hiring manager will perceive you as an
IC — even if your title says "Director."

This does **not** mean being vague. A high-altitude bullet is still concrete
and evidence-backed (see [Metrics and Quantification](metrics-and-quantification.md)).
It describes the *scope* of impact at the organizational level, with
specifics about what changed and for whom.

## The Narrow-Callout Trap

The most common altitude failure is the **narrow callout** — singling out one
specific technology or task as a key achievement when your actual impact was
a broader pattern of organizational change.

**Narrow (IC altitude):**

> Evangelized Kubernetes across the company.

This describes a single technology adoption. At executive altitude, the same
achievement is a **pattern of behavior** — catalyzing organizational adoption
of modern practices across multiple technology domains, of which Kubernetes
is one instance.

**Broad (enterprise altitude):**

> Catalyzed enterprise-wide adoption of modern technology practices across
> the company — championing Kubernetes, cloud-native architecture, and data
> platform modernization across 20+ business groups to enable business
> agility and scalable platform delivery.

The specific technology (Kubernetes) becomes one example of a broader
pattern. The bullet now communicates: "I drive organizational change across
multiple domains," not "I promoted one tool."

## How to Elevate Altitude

### 1. Identify the Pattern

Look at the specific achievement and ask: **What category of behavior does
this belong to?** If you evangelized Kubernetes, you likely also championed
other modern practices. The pattern is "catalyst for modern technology
practices," not "Kubernetes advocate."

### 2. List the Instances

Enumerate the specific instances of the pattern. If the pattern is "catalyst
for modern technology practices," the instances might be: Kubernetes,
cloud-native architecture, data platform modernization, database change
management.

### 3. Name the Business Outcome

High-altitude bullets must answer: **what changed at the organizational level
because of this pattern?** The outcome is not "we used Kubernetes" — it is
"the organization gained business agility and scalable platform delivery."

### 4. Scope the Impact

Quantify the organizational scope: how many teams, business groups, or
segments were affected? "Across 20+ business groups" is enterprise altitude.
"In my team" is team altitude.

### Transformation Table

| Narrow (low altitude) | Broad (high altitude) |
|-----------------------|----------------------|
| Evangelized Kubernetes across the company | Catalyzed enterprise-wide adoption of cloud-native and modern platform practices across 20+ business groups |
| Migrated the database to PostgreSQL | Drove the platform modernization initiative across the engineering org, replacing 3 legacy data stores with a unified cloud-native stack |
| Set up CI/CD pipelines | Established the engineering org's deployment infrastructure, reducing release cycle time from weeks to hours across 12 services |
| Wrote the API for the mobile app | Architected the company's mobile platform strategy, enabling 3 product teams to ship independently |

## When to Stay at Low Altitude

Not every bullet should be enterprise-altitude. Stay at implementation or
team altitude when:

- **The target role is IC-level** — A senior engineer resume should show
  technical depth, not just strategic breadth. An IC resume full of
  enterprise-altitude bullets with no implementation detail reads as
  "all strategy, no execution" (the Fluff Strategy anti-pattern from
  [Safe Pair of Hands Positioning](safe-pair-of-hands-positioning.md)).
- **The bullet is in a "proof of technical credibility" section** — One or
  two implementation-altitude bullets demonstrate that you can still do the
  work. The balance test from [Ownership Verbs](ownership-verbs.md) applies:
  technical credibility + business impact + leadership.
- **The achievement is genuinely narrow** — If you did one specific thing
  and it had one specific outcome, don't inflate it. Inflating a narrow
  achievement to enterprise altitude without evidence is fluff (see
  [Fluff and Buzzword Elimination](fluff-and-buzzword-elimination.md)).

## The Balance Test

A senior resume should show **altitude variety** — a mix of enterprise,
org, and team-altitude bullets that demonstrates both strategic vision and
execution capability. The mix shifts by target role:

| Target role | Enterprise | Org | Team | Implementation |
|-------------|-----------|-----|------|----------------|
| CTO / VP | 40-50% | 30-40% | 10-20% | 0-10% |
| Director | 20-30% | 40-50% | 20-30% | 0-10% |
| Senior IC | 0-10% | 10-20% | 40-50% | 30-40% |

If all your bullets are at one altitude, you have a positioning problem. An
all-enterprise resume looks like a strategist who can't execute. An
all-implementation resume looks like an IC who can't lead.

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- **[Ownership Verbs](ownership-verbs.md)** — The verb sets the altitude
  floor ("Led" is org altitude; "Helped" is implementation altitude). This
  concept extends the principle to the *scope* that follows the verb.
- **[Technology-Outcome Framing](technology-outcome-framing.md)** — When
  elevating altitude, frame technology as the means and business outcomes as
  the end. Don't just list more technologies at higher altitude.
- **[Safe Pair of Hands Positioning](safe-pair-of-hands-positioning.md)** —
  Altitude variety prevents the Builder Trap (all implementation) and the
  Fluff Strategy (all enterprise with no evidence).
- **[Metrics and Quantification](metrics-and-quantification.md)** —
  High-altitude bullets still need metrics. Scope the organizational impact
  with numbers (teams, business groups, revenue, cost).
